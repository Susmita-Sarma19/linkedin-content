# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-02

## Idea 1 — The Audience Problem

**Angle:** TV hosting taught her something digital marketers rarely learn — that "the audience" is never one person, and the moment you forget that, your content starts performing for itself instead of for anyone.

**Hook:** On television, you can feel when you've lost the room. Online, you can't. That's the problem.

**Outline:**
- In TV, the audience is immediate and physical — you adjust in real time or you fail visibly
- Digital content can "perform well" by metrics and still miss the person it was supposed to reach
- The discipline of speaking *to* someone (not *at* a demographic) is harder to maintain when you can't feel the room
- What she carries from the studio into content strategy: always ask who is actually on the other end

**Tone:** Quietly provocative

## Idea 2 — The Corridor Thank-You

**Angle:** The workplace moments that actually make people feel seen rarely show up in any recognition platform — and the gap between what we've built and what people actually need is worth sitting with.

**Hook:** The thank-you that changed someone's week wasn't in the system. It was said in the corridor on a Tuesday.

**Outline:**
- She works in HR tech — she believes in recognition tools — but she's also watched what moves people
- The most meaningful recognition often happens informally, unlogged, between two people
- This isn't an argument against platforms; it's an argument for not outsourcing the human part to software
- The question for managers: what are you doing in the corridors?

**Tone:** Warm and honest

## Idea 3 — The Code-Switch

**Angle:** Switching between four languages in a single workday has made her a sharper thinker in all of them — because translation forces you to strip a thought down to what it actually means.

**Hook:** Some mornings I've had four conversations in four different languages before lunch. Not because I had to. Because it's just Tuesday.

**Outline:**
- What code-switching actually feels like from the inside — not a party trick, a daily reality
- Each language has a different emotional register; switching trains you to notice the register you're in
- The professional insight: if you can't explain something in a second language, you probably don't understand it well enough in the first
- How this shows up in her writing: she drafts with one language in her head, edits with another

**Tone:** Curious, grounded
