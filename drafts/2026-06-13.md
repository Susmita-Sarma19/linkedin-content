# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-06-13

## Idea 1 — The Camera and the Cursor

**Angle:** What 9 years on a live TV set taught her about writing for digital audiences — commitment, pacing, and no safety net.

**Hook:** "The first time I went live on television, I realized there's no backspace key."

**Outline:**
- Live TV is unforgiving in a way no other medium is — you say it, it's out there, you move on
- That condition trained something: the instinct to commit to a thought before you open your mouth (or hit publish)
- Most digital content suffers from over-editing — it loses its voice trying to sound perfect
- What she carried from the studio into content strategy: reading a room you can't see, and trusting your instincts anyway

**Tone:** Punchy, specific

## Idea 2 — Built Without the Shortcut

**Angle:** What building a marketing career from Guwahati actually looks like — no hub proximity, no conference hallways, just the depth of the work.

**Hook:** "I've never run into a potential collaborator at a conference after-party. My city doesn't have those."

**Outline:**
- The unspoken infrastructure of metro careers: conference rooms, co-working serendipity, industry dinners — none of that is a given from Guwahati
- This isn't a complaint — it's an observation about what you build differently when proximity isn't an option
- You develop rigor. You develop an online voice. You let the work speak because nothing else will carry it
- The internet has started to level this, but the visibility bias hasn't fully caught up — and she's still waiting for it to

**Tone:** Candid, quietly defiant

## Idea 3 — The Cobbler's Shoes

**Angle:** The people who build recognition systems for others are often the least recognized themselves — an honest observation from inside HR tech.

**Hook:** "I spend a lot of time writing about how to make employees feel valued. I rarely think about whether the person writing this feels valued."

**Outline:**
- Working in HR tech means thinking about employee experience all day — recognition programs, engagement surveys, workplace culture
- There's a quiet irony: the teams running these programs (HR, content, operations) are often outside their own scope
- What this taught her about the difference between a system that recognizes and a moment that actually lands
- Recognition isn't a program. It's a practice — and it starts with noticing the people who are easy to overlook

**Tone:** Reflective, warm, gently wry
