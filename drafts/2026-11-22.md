# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-22

## Idea 1 — The Recognition Gap

**Angle:** Six years inside HR tech has shown her the difference between recognition that lands and recognition that disappears — and it's never the platform's fault.

**Hook:** We've made it easier than ever to recognize people at work. We haven't made it more meaningful.

**Outline:**
- The quiet irony: more tools, same hollowness — when recognition becomes a scheduled task, it stops feeling like recognition
- What she's watched happen: the moment a manager's specific, unscheduled words do more than any automated milestone notification
- The actual variable (it's specificity, not frequency — "you stayed to fix the problem nobody else noticed" vs. "great work this quarter")
- What HR tech can do well, and where it needs a human to actually show up

**Tone:** Quietly provocative

## Idea 2 — The Assumption of Leaving

**Angle:** Ambition that doesn't match the city's pace gets mistaken for lack of ambition — she stopped correcting people and started building instead.

**Hook:** People in Guwahati have asked me why I never moved. People in metro cities have asked me if I ever wanted to.

**Outline:**
- The unspoken equation: ambition = relocation, success = a different city
- What six years at one company in one city has actually looked like (depth, not stagnation — real knowledge of a specific market, real relationships, real institutional memory)
- The specific ways metro-centric career advice doesn't translate — when the playbook assumes you have three networking events a week and a co-working space downstairs
- Reframe: ambition is what you build, not where you go to build it

**Tone:** Self-assured, grounded

## Idea 3 — The Cut That Doesn't Make It

**Angle:** Nine years of television taught her that every piece of content is better for what it doesn't say — a discipline digital rarely rewards.

**Hook:** In nine years of television, I never once met a piece of content that couldn't be shorter.

**Outline:**
- What live TV enforces: you have the slot, the guest is there, the audience is watching — no room to ramble
- How digital inverted this: more space means more opportunity to hedge, over-explain, say the same thing twice
- The specific edit instinct she carried from broadcasting into every blog post, newsletter, and social caption she now writes
- The case for treating "shorter" as craft, not constraint — the reader knows when you trust them and when you don't

**Tone:** Crisp, quietly contrarian
