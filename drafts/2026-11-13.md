# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-13

## Idea 1 — The Person Behind the Post

**Angle:** She writes HR content for practitioners — but rarely names out loud who actually reads it, in what moment, and why that changes what good writing means.

**Hook:** I write about employee recognition. Someone reads it the week after a team member quit without saying why.

**Outline:**
- HR content is not consumed the way general business content is — it often gets read in reaction to something that just happened
- The specific reader: a manager mid-problem, not a professional browsing for ideas
- What it means to write for someone already in the room with the difficulty, not someone preparing for it
- The discipline that creates — precision over completeness, human over authoritative

**Tone:** Quietly purposeful

## Idea 2 — What Recognition Felt Like When It Happened to Me

**Angle:** She's written hundreds of pieces about employee recognition frameworks. The gap between how the frameworks describe recognition and what it actually feels like to receive it.

**Hook:** I've spent six years writing about how recognition works. When it happened to me, the frameworks didn't quite cover it.

**Outline:**
- The practitioner who studies something closely and then encounters it as a human — the dissonance is specific
- Not that the frameworks are wrong; it's that description and experience are different kinds of knowing
- What the felt experience of being recognized actually contains that the best-practice guides don't name
- What this teaches her about writing for the people who will design programs for others — the humility of the gap

**Tone:** Disarming, precise

## Idea 3 — Before Remote Work Was a Strategy

**Angle:** She's been building a career from Guwahati long before remote work became a movement — what the older experience of working from outside the industry's center reveals about what the conversation is still missing.

**Hook:** I've been working from outside the industry's center of gravity for over a decade. The remote work conversation is finally catching up to something I didn't know had a name.

**Outline:**
- The difference between "remote work" as a modern policy and the quieter, older experience of professional work from non-metro cities
- What it actually requires: building your own context, finding peer networks that don't form organically, being your own sounding board in the absence of ambient industry culture
- The assumption the remote work conversation doesn't name: "remote" implies a center to be remote from, and not every practitioner experiences it that way
- What professionals from Tier-2 cities have been learning to do quietly for years — and why that's not the same as flexibility

**Tone:** Grounded, gently corrective
