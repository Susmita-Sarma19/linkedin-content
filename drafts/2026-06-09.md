# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-06-09

## Idea 1 — The Two Postgraduates

**Angle:** Her MA in Mass Communication and PG Diploma in PR/Image Management trained her in two frameworks that fundamentally disagree — and that tension is the most useful thing she carries into HR content work.

**Hook:** "I have two postgraduate qualifications in communication. They've never agreed with each other."

**Outline:**
- Mass Comm's operating assumption: good content finds its audience. PR/Image Management's operating assumption: reception is not a direct function of quality — perception is its own system.
- The disagreement plays out specifically in HR work. A recognition program that genuinely works and one that's well-packaged but hollow can look identical from the outside. Telling the difference requires holding both frameworks at once.
- At Vantage Circle, the job isn't just to write well about employee engagement — it's to understand what an organization is actually doing before helping them say it. The image management training is what makes that distinction possible.
- She didn't study both on purpose. But the contradiction they generate turned out to be the thing she uses most.

**Tone:** Taut, a little wry

## Idea 2 — What the Numbers Don't Know

**Angle:** She writes employee engagement content calibrated to national benchmarks, from a city those benchmarks weren't built around — and the gap between the assumed employee and the actual one is one she knows from the inside.

**Hook:** "Every engagement benchmark I've ever written about was built on data from cities I've never lived in."

**Outline:**
- Her job is to make employees visible to their organizations. She works from Guwahati, writing for an HR audience across India, drawing on research calibrated to metro attrition rates, salary bands, and career ladder assumptions.
- What disengagement looks like in a Tier-2 city is structurally different — the reasons people stay, the reasons they leave, what "growth opportunity" actually means when the geography has fewer rungs. These don't always surface in the research she's working from.
- There's a specific irony: producing content about employee experience while recognizing that the version of the employee she's writing for often doesn't match the version that exists where she is.
- Not a complaint. An observation about whose experience gets operationalized in HR content — and why that gap matters for anyone trying to build recognition culture outside the metros.

**Tone:** Quietly unsettling, precise

## Idea 3 — The Five-Month Education

**Angle:** A short 2015 role helping Subway's development office map entry into Northeast and Eastern India taught her something about the gap between how a brand imagines a new market and how that market actually exists — a lesson that still runs every time she builds a content brief.

**Hook:** "In 2015, I spent five months helping a national brand figure out whether to enter a market it had mostly ignored. Their assumptions were wrong in specific, instructive ways."

**Outline:**
- Coordinator at Subway's development office for Northeast and Eastern India — expansion work, mapping a geography the brand hadn't systematically looked at. Five months.
- The gap between how a brand models a new market and what that market is actually like isn't usually a data failure. It's a failure of the questions being asked. Brands tend to evaluate new markets against their own expansion criteria; the market rarely fits the model cleanly.
- That kind of mismatch isn't unique to franchise expansion. It happens in content strategy, in HR communications, in any context where one group is trying to understand another they've previously abstracted.
- The five months were short. The habit of asking "who does this actually assume the audience is?" never left.

**Tone:** Analytical, with a quiet edge
