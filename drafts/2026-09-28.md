# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-09-28

## Idea 1 — The Rehearsal Room

**Angle:** What nine years of live TV taught her about sounding effortless — and why the best "spontaneous" content is almost never that.

**Hook:** The most natural-sounding things I've ever said on camera were rehearsed the hardest.

**Outline:**
- Live TV at Doordarshan had no retakes. You either showed up prepared or you showed up badly — in front of thousands.
- That pressure built a strange discipline: the more thorough the preparation, the freer you felt in the moment. Spontaneity is a product of structure, not the absence of it.
- This is the thing nobody tells you about content creation: the posts that read like casual thinking usually come from someone who thinks deeply and often.
- The takeaway isn't "work harder." It's that the invisible hours are what make the visible ones look easy.

**Tone:** Reflective, quietly instructive


## Idea 2 — When Recognition Isn't Recognition

**Angle:** A quiet critique from inside HR tech — companies that measure engagement but skip the thing that actually drives it.

**Hook:** I've spent years writing about employee recognition. I've also watched companies do it completely wrong.

**Outline:**
- There's a version of "recognition" that looks great on a dashboard and means almost nothing to the person receiving it. Points systems. Automated birthday messages. Announcements nobody reads.
- Real recognition is specific. It names the thing the person actually did. It comes from someone who noticed.
- Working in HR tech, she sees both sides: the intention behind the programs, and the gap between program and person.
- The question worth asking isn't "do we have a recognition program?" — it's "does our team feel seen?"

**Tone:** Warm, honest, slightly uncomfortable (in a good way)


## Idea 3 — The Distance Advantage

**Angle:** Building a career from Guwahati meant never living inside the echo chamber — and that turned out to be worth something.

**Hook:** I've never worked from a metro. I've also never had the luxury of assuming my audience thinks like me.

**Outline:**
- So much career advice is written from inside India's four or five major cities. The conversations, the benchmarks, the "trends" — they assume a certain kind of professional environment.
- Working from Guwahati means none of that was a given. The work had to travel further. The thinking had to account for more.
- There's a kind of clarity that comes from not being in the room where assumptions get made. You have to ask more questions. You have to check more things.
- Distance from the center isn't a disadvantage dressed up nicely. It genuinely changes how you see problems — and sometimes that's the sharper vantage point.

**Tone:** Confident, grounded, gently contrarian
