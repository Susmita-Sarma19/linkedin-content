# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-10-01

## Idea 1 — The October Spreadsheet

**Angle:** Q4 is when recognition budgets get reviewed — but the employees who needed recognizing have been keeping their own tally since January.

**Hook:** October 1 means Q4 has started. In most companies, it also means someone just opened the recognition budget spreadsheet.

**Outline:**
- The pattern: year-end scramble to hit engagement targets, recognize teams, tick the boxes before performance review season closes
- What employees actually notice: sustained, specific acknowledgment throughout the year vs the burst of appreciation that arrives when it's strategically convenient
- She writes about recognition for a living — which means she also writes the content companies use during exactly this Q4 surge. There's a useful discomfort in that.
- The question she comes back to: what does it mean to recognize someone's work, and does it mean something different when a calendar reminded you to do it?

**Tone:** Dry, honest, a little uncomfortable

## Idea 2 — The Accent I Edited Out

**Angle:** Growing up multilingual in Northeast India meant code-switching before she had a word for it — and unlearning that erasure was the actual professional development.

**Hook:** My Hindi sounds like Assam. My English sounds like someone who learned it through books. For a long time I tried to edit both out of my professional voice.

**Outline:**
- What regional accent-editing looks like in practice: the softened vowel, the over-corrected grammar, the careful monitoring of how you sound to a metro audience that didn't ask to be catered to
- The Doordarshan years changed this — broadcasting in Assamese and Hindi for a local audience where the "correct" accent was already her accent; nobody was waiting for her to sound like Delhi
- The transfer to digital marketing: writing for a pan-India HR audience meant confronting the same instinct again. Who decides what professional sounds like?
- What she kept when she stopped editing: specificity. The Guwahati perspective inside the content. The reader who also didn't go to a metro school and is tired of content that assumes they did.

**Tone:** Candid, quietly defiant

## Idea 3 — The Post That Didn't Rank

**Angle:** The piece her analytics said failed is the one a practitioner emailed her about six months later — and she's still working out which signal to trust.

**Hook:** The best thing I wrote last year barely ranked. I know because I checked, and then I checked again, hoping the number would change.

**Outline:**
- She publishes HR content for Vantage Circle — which means SEO metrics, readership data, time-on-page are all visible and all matter for her job
- One piece underperformed every benchmark. No search traffic, low shares, quiet as far as the dashboard was concerned.
- Six months later: an email from an HR manager in Pune who said it changed how she structured her next recognition program. She'd found it through a colleague who'd printed it out.
- The question this creates: what is "performing" actually measuring? And how do you keep writing toward the email when the dashboard exists?

**Tone:** Reflective, a little wry
