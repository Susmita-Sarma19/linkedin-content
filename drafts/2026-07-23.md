# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-23

## Idea 1 — The Audience Read

**Angle:** Nine years of live television taught a kind of audience intelligence that no analytics dashboard can replicate — and it quietly became the foundation of everything she does in digital.

**Hook:** "Before I had a dashboard, I had a camera. And a camera teaches you things about attention that no spreadsheet ever will."

**Outline:**
- In live TV, there's no buffering — you know in real time if you've lost the room, and you can't A/B test your way out of it
- That instinct — reading what's landing before anyone tells you — turned out to be the most transferable skill she carried into digital marketing
- Data tells you what happened; the read tells you why it's about to happen
- The oldest skill in communication is still the most underrated in digital work

**Tone:** Reflective, quietly confident

## Idea 2 — The Recognition Paradox

**Angle:** Working inside the employee recognition industry for six years has made her a sharper, more honest observer of the gap between what companies think recognition is and what it actually does to people.

**Hook:** "I've spent six years helping companies think about employee recognition. That's made me very hard to impress — and a lot more curious about what actually works."

**Outline:**
- Recognition programs and recognition moments are not the same thing — one is designed, one is felt
- What employees remember is almost never what the program intended them to remember
- The difference between being acknowledged and being seen — and why conflating them is a quiet mistake
- What six years inside this space has shifted in how she thinks about her own work and what she asks for

**Tone:** Honest, perceptive, dry without being cynical

## Idea 3 — Your Own Benchmark

**Angle:** When there's no one in your city doing what you do, you stop measuring yourself against peers — and start building an internal standard that's harder to game and harder to lose.

**Hook:** "For years, I was the only person I knew personally who did what I did. No peer group. No meetups. No 'let's grab coffee and talk shop.'"

**Outline:**
- What professional isolation actually looks like when you're building a digital career in a Tier-2 city — not dramatic, just quiet
- How the absence of local comparison forced a different kind of accountability: you become your own benchmark
- The unexpected clarity that comes from not having a reference point — you stop asking "am I doing this right?" and start asking "is this the right thing to do?"
- Why she now tries to be visible in the way she wished someone had been visible for her

**Tone:** Warm, honest, quietly empowering — no sermon
