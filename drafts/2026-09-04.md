# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-09-04

## Idea 1 — The Overlap Years

**Angle:** For three-plus years (September 2019–December 2022), she was simultaneously a practicing TV talk show host at Doordarshan and a digital marketing professional at Vantage Circle — not transitioning between them, but genuinely both at once, in two rooms that knew almost nothing about each other.

**Hook:** For three years, I had two completely different professional identities running at the same time. Nobody in either room knew much about the other one.

**Outline:**
- Monday: analyzing SEO performance for an HR tech platform. Some weeks: live on air at a national broadcaster, interviewing guests, holding a room.
- She wasn't "transitioning" — she was coexisting. Two different versions of what it meant to communicate professionally, operating in parallel.
- Each role required you to leave the other at the door. The discipline of that — of being fully one thing in each room — is something you can only learn by doing it.
- The question she keeps returning to: which one changed the other more?

**Tone:** Quietly curious

## Idea 2 — Coming Back

**Angle:** After two years in Kolkata — her first sustained experience of a metro professional environment — she returned to Guwahati and has built her career from there since. Most career narratives go toward the center. She went, saw it, and came back. That's its own arc.

**Hook:** I lived in Kolkata for two years. I came back to Guwahati and have been here since. I'm still not sure if that was a decision I made or just what happened.

**Outline:**
- releaseMyAd.com, Kolkata, 2015–2017: her first exposure to a metro advertising environment — a different pace, a different professional network, a different relationship between work and the city around it.
- Returning meant trading what the metro offers (density, proximity, visible career infrastructure) for what home offers — which is harder to name but also harder to replicate elsewhere.
- Building a career in the city you grew up in is not the same as not leaving. You carry the comparison with you. It becomes a reference point, not an escape.
- The return as a recurring theme in Northeast career paths: the professional who goes out, acquires something, and brings it back — versus the one who stays and wonders whether they should have gone.

**Tone:** Honest and grounded

## Idea 3 — Image Management Taught Me This

**Angle:** Her PG Diploma in PR and Image Management (Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan, 2014–2015) was training in how organizations construct and protect their public identity — but the most interesting application has been internal: how employee recognition functions as reputation management directed inward, at your own people.

**Hook:** I studied image management at a time when I thought the audience you managed your image for was always outside the organization. Six years writing about recognition changed that assumption completely.

**Outline:**
- PR and image management is about controlling the story an organization tells about itself to external audiences — press, public, stakeholders.
- What she discovered writing about employee recognition: the most consequential version of that story is the one employees tell themselves about whether the place they work sees them accurately.
- Recognition isn't HR housekeeping. It's the company's most direct communication to its own people about what it believes they're worth. That's image management from the inside.
- The diploma and the job met in a place she didn't expect: the audience that shapes organizational reputation most durably is the one that shows up every morning.

**Tone:** Reframing, quietly confident
