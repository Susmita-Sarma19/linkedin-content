# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-09-04

## Idea 1 — The Five-Year Detour

**Angle:** Before Mass Communication, before Doordarshan, before digital marketing — there were five years studying business. The B.Com credential she stepped away from is likely doing structural work in everything she does now.

**Hook:** Five years of studying business. One degree I've barely mentioned since. Turns out the "wrong" education doesn't disappear — it just changes how it shows up.

**Outline:**
- BCom at Gauhati Commerce College (2007–2012): five years in accounting, business management, commerce fundamentals, before an abrupt turn to Mass Communication.
- From the outside it looks like an abandoned investment. But the business frame didn't stay behind — it shows up in how she reads a brief that's really a revenue problem, or maps content to a business objective.
- Business logic wasn't a skill she picked up later. It was her first five years. It arrived before the storytelling did.
- What you learn first is often what you reach for when the problem is hard — not from nostalgia, but because sequence shapes how you see things.

**Tone:** Quietly reframing

## Idea 2 — The Market Study on My Hometown

**Angle:** Her first Kolkata job was helping evaluate whether Northeast India was worth entering as a market. She was from Northeast India. She took the job — and discovered that being the insider in the room doesn't automatically mean being heard.

**Hook:** My first Kolkata job was helping a sandwich chain decide whether Northeast India was worth entering. I'm from Northeast India. I took the job.

**Outline:**
- Subway Systems India, Kolkata, 2015: five months assessing franchise viability and market potential for the Northeast and Eastern India region.
- She knew what the data would miss — the actual texture of the region, the infrastructure reality, the context a number can't carry.
- Being the person with the most relevant knowledge in the room and still having to translate it into the vocabulary the decision-makers were using: that's its own discipline.
- The question it lodged — who gets to define what a place is worth — has never fully left.

**Tone:** Pointed and grounded

## Idea 3 — Going Back

**Angle:** She left Guwahati, built something real in Kolkata, and then went home — and built her actual career from there. The standard narrative around this frames it as retreat. She's not convinced that's right.

**Hook:** Most professionals who make it in Kolkata don't go back to Guwahati. I did. I'm still not entirely sure what to call that.

**Outline:**
- The Kolkata years: PG diploma, Subway, releaseMyAd.com — three years building professional foundation in the nearest metro.
- Then home: SmartCoach in 2018, Vantage Circle from 2019. Her entire digital marketing career built from the city she grew up in.
- The retreat narrative vs. the defensively positive "I chose home and it was enough" counter-narrative — and why neither quite fits.
- Community, language, context, knowing the room before you walk in — these aren't consolation prizes. They're the actual conditions of a life.

**Tone:** Honest, quietly firm

## Idea 4 — The Overlap Years

**Angle:** From September 2019 to December 2022, she was simultaneously a practicing TV talk show host at Doordarshan and a digital marketing professional at Vantage Circle — not transitioning between them, but genuinely both at once. Nobody in either room knew much about the other one.

**Hook:** For three years, I had two completely different professional identities running at the same time. Nobody in either room knew much about the other one.

**Outline:**
- Monday: analyzing SEO performance for an HR tech platform. Some weeks: live on air at a national broadcaster, holding a conversation in real time with a guest she'd just met.
- She wasn't transitioning — she was coexisting. Two different modes of what it meant to communicate professionally, running in parallel.
- Each role required leaving the other at the door. The discipline of being fully one thing in each room is something you only learn by doing it simultaneously.
- Which one changed the other more? She's still working that out.

**Tone:** Quietly curious

## Idea 5 — Image Management Taught Me This

**Angle:** Her PG Diploma in PR and Image Management was training in how organizations construct their public identity. Six years writing about employee recognition revealed where the most important version of that work actually happens: inside the organization, not outside it.

**Hook:** I studied image management at a time when I thought the audience you managed your image for was always outside the organization. Six years writing about recognition changed that assumption completely.

**Outline:**
- PR and image management: controlling the story an organization tells about itself to external audiences — press, public, stakeholders.
- What she discovered writing about employee recognition: the most consequential version of that story is the one employees tell themselves about whether the place they work sees them accurately.
- Recognition isn't HR housekeeping. It's the company's most direct communication to its own people about what it believes they're worth.
- The diploma and the job met in a place she didn't expect: the audience that shapes organizational reputation most durably is the one that shows up every morning.

**Tone:** Reframing, quietly confident
