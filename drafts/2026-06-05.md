# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-06-05

## Idea 1 — The Return

**Angle:** She left Guwahati for Kolkata in 2015, did what career advice prescribes, and came back — not in defeat, but having answered the question she was actually asking.

**Hook:** "I moved to Kolkata in 2015. I came back to Guwahati in 2018. Most people who heard that story understood it as a retreat. It wasn't."

**Outline:**
- Two years inside an industry cluster: what proximity to a metro actually gives you — colleagues, access, the hum of adjacency — and what it doesn't
- The specific knowledge that comes from standing somewhere else long enough to feel the comparison; you can't know where your best work happens until you've tested the alternative
- The return as a conclusion, not a consolation: Guwahati became a position because she had stood somewhere else first
- What "going back" looks like to the people who stayed, and why neither reading is quite right

**Tone:** Grounded, quietly defiant, specific

## Idea 2 — The Image Manager's Blind Spot

**Angle:** She has a PG diploma in PR and image management — and it took her ten years to aim those tools at her own career.

**Hook:** "I can tell you exactly how perception is shaped, how trust is built, how a professional reputation travels. That's what I trained in. It took me ten years to aim those tools at my own career."

**Outline:**
- The academic foundation: understanding the mechanics of how institutions and people are perceived — her actual training
- Spent the following decade applying that logic to other people's experiences: employee recognition, workplace visibility, how companies make people feel seen
- What she didn't do for a long time: run the same analysis on herself — not negligence, but expertise in a domain and applying that expertise to your own case are genuinely different skills
- The cobbler's children problem has a specific shape: it's not that you don't know. It's that knowing is not the same as looking.

**Tone:** Self-aware, a little wry, honest without being confessional

## Idea 3 — The Formal Ending

**Angle:** She officially left Doordarshan in December 2022 — three years after she had, practically, already moved on.

**Hook:** "On paper, I worked at Doordarshan for nine years and five months. The part the resume doesn't capture: for the last three of those years, I'd already moved on."

**Outline:**
- Joined Vantage Circle in September 2019; the Doordarshan tenure officially closed in December 2022 — three years of overlap that look different depending on which end you read from
- The transition narrative: careful, one foot on familiar ground while testing new territory; the ending narrative: December 2022 was when the paperwork caught up to something already true
- How professional endings actually work — not events, but processes with a final administrative step at the far end
- The post doesn't resolve into a lesson; it ends where the observation does: endings have dates, but they rarely happen on them

**Tone:** Understated, honest, quietly revelatory

## Idea 4 — Speaking Last

**Angle:** A habit that looks like passivity — holding your position in meetings until the room has spoken — is actually a specific kind of authority, wired in by nine years of live television.

**Hook:** "I almost always speak last in meetings. It took me years to stop apologizing for that."

**Outline:**
- Nine years of hosting trained one reflex above all others: listen for the full shape of the thing before you name it — you don't frame the segment until you know where the guest is going
- In professional meetings, speaking late gets misread easily: hesitant, unprepared, waiting to be told what to think
- What it actually does: by the time you go, you have better information, your position has already accounted for the room, and you're not defending a stake you planted before the conversation started
- What it builds over time: people know your contribution lands after theirs — not before, not over, after — and that specific quality of listening creates a different kind of trust

**Tone:** Quietly confident, counterintuitive, grounded

## Idea 5 — The Brief That Came Back Wrong

**Angle:** The marketing brief that completely misread the audience taught more about HR managers as readers than any successful campaign — failure is the most honest data source.

**Hook:** "The piece I'm proudest of learning from is one almost nobody read. It came from a brief that assumed it already knew the audience."

**Outline:**
- The brief was technically sound: clear objective, defined persona, right format — and the content it produced landed flat, barely opened, never forwarded
- What the non-response actually revealed: the assumed pain point wasn't the live one; the persona was accurate on paper but hadn't been built from what HR managers were actually carrying that quarter
- Reverse-engineering the audience from the gap: what you expected to resonate, what didn't, and what that distance tells you about the distance between your model of a reader and the real one
- You don't learn who you're writing for from the strategy deck — you learn it from the moment the connection doesn't happen and you have to be honest about why

**Tone:** Specific, craftsperson, self-aware without being confessional

## Idea 6 — What Recognition Actually Looks Like

**Angle:** Six years writing frameworks for employee recognition — and the moment at work that has personally mattered most didn't fit any of them.

**Hook:** "I've spent six years writing frameworks for employee recognition. The moment at work that has stayed with me longest didn't fit any of them."

**Outline:**
- The frameworks are correct: timely, specific, meaningful, visible — all worth teaching, all backed by real research
- But the moment that actually landed for her was quieter than any system would have produced — not tied to a milestone, not on a platform, not witnessed by anyone who wasn't there
- What made it land wasn't the structure around it: it was that the person giving it had genuinely seen her work — not the deliverable, not the output, but the thinking underneath it
- The implication she keeps sitting with: maybe the most useful thing she can write about recognition isn't the framework — it's helping people develop the capacity to actually see someone else's work before they try to name it

**Tone:** Reflective, honest, slightly unsettling in the best way
