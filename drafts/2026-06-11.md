# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-06-11

## Idea 1 — The Audience Test

**Angle:** Nine years of live television trained her to read rooms before she ever knew what "audience segmentation" meant — and that reflex turned out to be the most transferable skill she owns.

**Hook:** Going live in front of a studio audience teaches you one thing fast: there is no hiding behind a screen.

**Outline:**
- In live TV, you feel the room shift in real time — attention rises, energy drops, people look away. You adjust before you lose them, not after.
- Digital marketing gives you data after the fact. But the instinct to sense your audience before the numbers tell you — that's not something a dashboard teaches.
- Most content creators optimize for clicks. What broadcasting trained her to optimize for was presence — are you still with me?
- The skill transferred. The medium changed. The core question stayed the same.

**Tone:** Quietly confident, a little surprising

## Idea 2 — The Recognition Gap

**Angle:** From inside HR tech, there's a pattern she keeps seeing: companies build recognition programs around what looks good in dashboards, not what employees actually feel.

**Hook:** A lot of recognition programs feel like they were built to close a survey gap, not to make someone feel seen.

**Outline:**
- What companies often mean by recognition: points, badges, leaderboards, public shoutouts timed to quarterly reviews.
- What employees actually want: someone noticing the specific thing, at the right moment, without waiting for a system prompt.
- The irony: the more automated recognition becomes, the less it feels like recognition. Efficiency is not the same as sincerity.
- The best managers she's observed don't wait for the program. They just pay attention.

**Tone:** Observational, gently pointed

## Idea 3 — The Tier-2 Advantage

**Angle:** Most Indian workplace content is written from metros, for metros — and it quietly encodes assumptions that don't hold for most of the country. Writing from Guwahati means writing without those assumptions, and that makes the work more honest.

**Hook:** Most workplace content in India is written in Mumbai or Bangalore. Most Indian workers are not in Mumbai or Bangalore.

**Outline:**
- "Universal" workplace advice often comes loaded: long commutes as the default, hybrid culture as the norm, metro salary benchmarks as the baseline.
- That's not what work looks like for most people in this country. It's a narrow slice presented as the whole picture.
- Writing HR and marketing content from a Tier-2 city means constantly asking: does this actually hold outside a metro? Often, it doesn't.
- That question isn't a limitation. It's an editorial instinct most content doesn't have.

**Tone:** Clear-eyed, quietly provocative
