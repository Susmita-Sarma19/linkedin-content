# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-06-01

## Idea 1 — The 30-Second Rule

**Angle:** Nine years of live television taught a specific, unsentimental lesson about attention — one that most digital content ignores entirely.

**Hook:** You learn very quickly on live TV: if you lose the audience in the first 30 seconds, you've lost them. Digital content is no different. We've just forgotten it.

**Outline:**
- On live TV there are no retakes — attention is earned in real time, and the pressure sharpens your instincts fast
- How she applies the "first 30 seconds" principle when writing content and scripting podcast openings
- Why most B2B content fails — it assumes the reader is already interested, rather than earning that interest
- The difference between expecting attention and actually earning it: what that shift looks like in practice

**Tone:** Precise, quietly provocative

## Idea 2 — Recognition Is Not a Reward Program

**Angle:** The category error companies keep making — confusing incentive programs with the entirely human act of being seen.

**Hook:** A gift voucher on your work anniversary is not recognition. It's a transaction.

**Outline:**
- What recognition actually means versus what most companies call recognition
- The gap between being rewarded and being seen — why they feel completely different to the person receiving them
- Why employees disengage even when the perks look generous on paper
- What it looks like when recognition lands right: specific, timely, personal — not templated

**Tone:** Direct, a little challenging, grounded in years of HR content work

## Idea 3 — Building Digital Without the Ecosystem

**Angle:** What it's actually like to build a digital marketing career in a city where no such ecosystem exists — and what that absence quietly teaches you.

**Hook:** There was no digital marketing community in Guwahati when I started. No meetups, no industry events, no one to ask over coffee. So I figured it out the slow way — which turned out to be the better way.

**Outline:**
- What the Tier-2 reality looks like for someone building skills in a field the city doesn't yet have a name for
- How the absence of a ready ecosystem forces a different kind of discipline — you stop performing for a local scene and just do the work
- The unexpected upside: no echo chambers, no groupthink, no "this is just how it's done here"
- Why some of the sharpest digital thinkers she's met built their careers far from the metros — and what that says about how we talk about talent

**Tone:** Warm, grounded, quietly affirming — not a complaint, a reframe
