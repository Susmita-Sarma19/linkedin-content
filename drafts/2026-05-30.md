## Idea 1 — The Simultaneous Lives

### Hook
"For three years, I had two jobs. One was live television. One was digital marketing. They shared a calendar but almost nothing else."

### Angle
From late 2019 to late 2022, she held both roles at the same time — Talk Show Host at Doordarshan and Digital Marketing Specialist at Vantage Circle. The disciplines pull in opposite directions: broadcasting is live, singular, one-directional, uneditable. Digital is iterative, measurable, and built to be refined. Holding both required a constant mental gear-shift that, in retrospect, made each job sharper. The TV work kept her from treating content as a performance metric. The content work gave her analytical language for what the TV work was actually doing. The post ends not on a productivity lesson but on a quieter observation: carrying two professional identities that have never met each other gives you a detachment from both — and detachment, sometimes, is where the clearest thinking happens.

### Source
Profile.md — Talk Show Host, Doordarshan Kendra (Aug 2013–Dec 2022) overlapping with Senior Digital Marketing Specialist, Vantage Circle (Sep 2019–Jun 2025). A three-year period where both roles were simultaneously active. The overlap is visible in the dates but has never been the subject of any idea in the drafts or the CLAUDE.md ideas bank.

### Resonance
Anyone who has held parallel professional lives — creative practice alongside a day job, freelance alongside full-time — will recognise the specific texture of managing two identities that don't talk to each other. It also complicates the common "pivot" narrative: her move from TV to digital wasn't a clean transition. It was a long overlap. That's a more honest story.

## Idea 2 — The First Audience Research

### Hook
"My first lesson about whether content actually reaches people happened in rural Guwahati, door to door, with a clipboard."

### Angle
In September 2013, she was a surveyor on an IIMC-led research project studying how Bharat Nirman government advertisements reached — or didn't reach — rural audiences. Walking door-to-door, asking real people what they'd seen and remembered, she encountered the gap between what is broadcast and what is received for the first time. No dashboard captures this. No engagement metric measures it. The experience became the original calibration point for everything she would later do in digital: a field-level understanding that reach is a delivery claim, not a reception fact. Thirteen years later, writing for HR managers trying to communicate to employees they may rarely see, the question from 2013 hasn't changed. It's just wearing different clothes.

### Source
Profile.md — Surveyor, Research Campaign on Reach and Access of Bharat Nirman Advertisements (Under IIMC, New Delhi), September 2013. One month, rural Guwahati area. The earliest documented professional experience in the profile, and completely unexplored across all prior drafts and the ideas bank.

### Resonance
Communication professionals who have learned the hard way that "sent" doesn't mean "received" will find the origin story both specific and credible. For her HR practitioner audience, the parallel to internal communications that travel but don't land will be immediate — and the framing of this as a career-long question, not a one-off lesson, gives it weight.

## Idea 3 — Two Months Behind, Nine Years In Front

### Hook
"I spent two months learning how a TV show was made. Then I spent nine years being the one it was made around. The order mattered."

### Angle
In June–July 2013, she was a production intern at Doordarshan Kendra. Two months later, she was on-air as the host. What she saw from the production side — how the floor director managed pace, what made a segment drag before the edit touched it, how the talent looked to the crew when nobody was performing for anyone — became a second perspective she never lost once she stepped in front of the camera. Most hosts see their work from inside the performance. She had seen it from outside first. The post explores what that means in practice: the awareness that the segment serves the show, not the other way around; that the viewer is somewhere else, tired and only half-watching; that the moment you stop thinking about the audience and start thinking about how you're coming across, you can feel the room go flat. The reflection lands on a wider observation: seeing yourself from the outside before you're at the centre of something changes what the centre feels like.

### Source
Profile.md — Production Intern, Doordarshan Kendra, Guwahati (June–July 2013), immediately followed by Talk Show Host, Doordarshan Kendra (August 2013–December 2022). The near-instant transition from intern to on-air host — two months — is a specific biographical detail with clear creative meaning. Not touched in any prior draft or the ideas bank.

### Resonance
Anyone who has held both sides of a creative operation — production and performance, management and individual contribution, editing and writing — will recognise the particular value of having stood in both places. The insight that seeing the outside view first changes how you inhabit the inside is general enough to land across industries, specific enough to feel earned.
