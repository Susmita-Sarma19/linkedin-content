# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-01

## Idea 1 — The Quiet Mid-Year

**Angle:** July 1st as a moment of honest inventory, not a hustle-culture scoreboard reset — what actually accumulated in the first half of the year without being tracked.

**Hook:** Somewhere around mid-year, I stop counting what's left and start noticing what accumulated.

**Outline:**
- The LinkedIn version of July 1st — the countdown, the "H2 let's go", the recalculation — and why it exhausts rather than motivates
- What actually happened in the first six months: not the goals, but the ground shifts you didn't plan for
- The professional things that moved quietly: a relationship deepened, a skill clicked, a piece of work that surprised you
- Inventory isn't the same as achievement. Both are worth doing. Only one is honest about what the year actually held.

**Tone:** Calm, gently contrarian

## Idea 2 — Working in Recognition

**Angle:** Six years in employee recognition technology — coming from a medium that never once told her whether any of it landed.

**Hook:** I've spent six years working on how organizations make people feel seen. I spent nine years before that in a medium that never told me if I was.

**Outline:**
- What recognition looks like from the inside of an HR tech company: frameworks, research, program design, the science of what makes acknowledgment feel real vs. performative
- What nine years at Doordarshan taught about working without that loop — the discipline of making something carefully when the audience is entirely invisible
- The thing you discover when you study recognition professionally: the mechanics are well understood, the feeling is not
- What the two halves of her career, together, clarify about why recognition fails in organizations — and what it actually needs to do

**Tone:** Quietly ironic, precise

## Idea 3 — The Degree Nobody Asks About

**Angle:** Her Post-Graduate Diploma in PR and Image Management from Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan — and what public relations training gave her that content marketing rarely talks about.

**Hook:** I have two postgraduate qualifications. One comes up constantly. The other one is the one that actually runs how I think.

**Outline:**
- The MA in Mass Communication: the one that fits the narrative, the one she leads with
- The PG Diploma in PR and Image Management from Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan: what it actually covered — audience perception, reputation management, the relationship between message and meaning
- How PR training is different from content training: it teaches you to think about what the audience already believes before you say anything
- How this shows up in employer branding, HR communication, recognition content — every piece of organizational writing is a reputation act whether the writer knows it or not

**Tone:** Revelatory, grounded
