# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-05

## Idea 1 — The City That Stayed

**Angle:** Choosing not to relocate from Guwahati was a professional decision, not just a personal one — and it shaped a kind of expertise the metro circuit doesn't produce.

**Hook:** Every few years, someone asks when I'm moving to a bigger city. I still haven't come up with a better answer than: I'm already in one.

**Outline:**
- The assumption embedded in that question — that career growth requires a physical move to a metro center
- What Guwahati actually provided: proximity to organizations navigating real constraints, relationships that conference-circuit networking can't replicate, a pace that lets you think before you write
- How the Northeast perspective shows up in her work without being announced — the implicit diversity of what "the workplace" means across India, what employee engagement looks like outside the Bangalore or Mumbai reference frame
- The difference between being rooted somewhere and being limited by it

**Tone:** quietly provocative

## Idea 2 — The Product I Actually Believe In

**Angle:** Marketing employee recognition software when you personally believe people deserve to feel seen at work is different from marketing by brief — and that alignment has a cost as well as a gift.

**Hook:** Some months, the most honest thing I can say about my work is that it matters. I've been trying to figure out what that costs me.

**Outline:**
- What it feels like to market a product whose core premise — employees deserve to feel valued — is also your own
- When the alignment is clean: the content that writes itself because the conviction doesn't have to be performed
- When the alignment creates friction: the optimization brief vs. the people-first instinct; the metric that says the campaign worked vs. the quieter question of whether the recognition actually landed for the person who received it
- What you owe your audience when you believe what you're selling — a different standard than when you've simply been briefed to sell it

**Tone:** honest, reflective

## Idea 3 — What Gets Left on the Floor

**Angle:** Editing a podcast episode is an argument about what the conversation actually was — and ninety minutes in the edit teaches you something that ninety minutes of recording doesn't.

**Hook:** The best thing a guest said in our last episode didn't make the final cut. I've been thinking about it since.

**Outline:**
- What the editing process reveals: the gap between what someone said and what they were actually trying to say
- Patterns she notices after dozens of edits — the false starts that are more honest than the finished sentences; the tangent that contains the real insight; the moment a guest stopped performing and started thinking out loud
- How editing other people's speech changed the way she listens — in an interview, yes, but also in an ordinary conversation
- The edit as a theory of meaning: what you cut is as much of an argument as what you keep, and knowing that changes what you hear the first time around

**Tone:** craft-focused, quietly philosophical
