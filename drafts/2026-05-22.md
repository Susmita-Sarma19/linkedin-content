## Idea 1: The Parallel Years

### Hook
"I never told my TV audience I had a day job. I never told my day job I was on TV."

### Angle
From 2019 to 2022, Susmita ran two full professional lives in parallel — Talk Show Host at Doordarshan Kendra and digital marketing at Vantage Circle. Not a side hustle. Two different communication registers, two different identities, running simultaneously.

The post explores what each taught the other: broadcast media instilled instincts about presence, pacing, and holding an audience; content analytics gave her a way to measure what actually landed. It ends on the quiet decision to leave Doordarshan at the end of 2022 — not because she failed at it, but because one identity had grown enough to stand on its own. Leaving something after nine years, by choice, is its own kind of milestone.

The tone is reflective, not nostalgic. No hustle narrative. Just the honest texture of what it's like to hold two professional selves at once and decide, calmly, to let one go.

### Source
Profile.md — Doordarshan Talk Show Host (Aug 2013–Dec 2022); Vantage Circle starts Sep 2019. Three-plus years of direct overlap.

### Resonance
Multi-hyphenate professionals rarely talk about the logistics and psychology of parallel identities. This post names what many people experience but don't articulate — and the ending (choosing to leave on your own terms) is quietly powerful.

## Idea 2: The Survey in the Field

### Hook
"My first real media job wasn't in a studio. It was walking door to door in rural Assam, asking people if they'd seen the government's ads."

### Angle
One month into her Mass Communication studies at Gauhati University, Susmita did field research for IIMC New Delhi — measuring whether Bharat Nirman government ad campaigns actually reached rural audiences in Guwahati. Most hadn't seen them.

That gap — between what content creators assume and what audiences actually receive — became a formative lens. She was trained to make media, and this was her first encounter with the distance between production and reception. A decade later, she writes HR content and runs digital campaigns. The fieldwork is still in there: every piece she writes carries the awareness that reach is not the same as resonance.

The post is a meditation on origin points — the unglamorous early experience that quietly shapes everything after it.

### Source
Profile.md — "Research Campaign on Reach and Access of Bharat Nirman Advertisements (Under IIMC, New Delhi), Surveyor, September 2013, Guwahati (rural)"

### Resonance
Content strategists who've done the hard work of measuring actual reach — not just impressions — see the craft differently. This is a rare origin story for a digital marketer. It's also a gentle provocation for anyone in communications to ask: are we actually reaching the people we think we are?

## Idea 3: The Kolkata Chapter

### Hook
"Kolkata taught me what I didn't want. Guwahati taught me what I did."

### Angle
After her PG Diploma in PR and Image Management, Susmita took a media relations role at a Kolkata-based company — a genuine entry into metro media industry infrastructure. She stayed two years. Then she came back to Guwahati.

The post is not about why Kolkata didn't work. It's about the moment she realized the most interesting version of her career was the one she'd build at home, not the one that fit the pre-existing template. Coming back set the stage for everything: the Doordarshan years, the Vantage Circle chapter, the work she does now. The decision to return wasn't a retreat — it was a bet on herself in a place that rarely had the infrastructure to make it easy.

It ends with a question for the reader: what if the detour you took away from home was actually the research that told you why to go back?

### Source
Profile.md — releaseMyAd.com Media Relations Associate (Aug 2015–Jul 2017, Kolkata); followed by SmartCoach in Guwahati (Jan 2018), then Vantage Circle (2019).

### Resonance
Professionals from smaller cities and towns face the "stay or leave for the metro" question in silence. This post names it directly and offers a resolution that isn't about failure or limitation — it's about a deliberate, formative choice. For her Northeast audience especially, this will land.
