# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-10-21

## Idea 1 — The Silence Lesson

**Angle:** Nine years in front of a live camera taught her the most underrated communication skill — knowing when to stop talking.

**Hook:** The best interview I ever did on Doordarshan, I barely said anything.

**Outline:**
- The instinct on live TV is to fill silence. Silence feels like failure. It took years to unlearn that.
- The guest was the story. My job was to create space, not occupy it.
- She carries this into content work: the post that says less often lands harder. The campaign that doesn't over-explain the audience more credit.
- Most content today is afraid of quiet. We stuff every paragraph, every caption, every ad. What if restraint was a strategy?

**Tone:** Reflective, quietly instructive

## Idea 2 — The Recognition Paradox

**Angle:** She builds content about employee recognition for a living — and has noticed how rarely the companies talking loudest about it practice it internally.

**Hook:** I write about employee recognition every day. It's made me a more careful observer of the places that don't.

**Outline:**
- Working in HR tech means absorbing a lot of research: what makes people feel valued, what happens when they don't.
- But the more she learned about recognition, the more she noticed the gap between what organizations publish and what they do.
- It's not cynicism — it's a useful tension. The gap between stated values and lived culture is where the real work lives.
- The question she sits with: does the content she creates push that gap smaller, or just add more noise?

**Tone:** Honest, slightly uncomfortable — the good kind

## Idea 3 — The Tier-2 Clarity

**Angle:** Being based in Guwahati, outside every major industry hub, isn't a handicap — it's a filter for what actually matters.

**Hook:** Most of my industry conversations happen with people 2,000 km away. That distance has been one of my biggest advantages.

**Outline:**
- When you're not in the room where the trends are born, you're forced to ask: does this actually work, or does it just sound good in a conference?
- Guwahati is not a city that mistakes buzz for substance. The audience here is practical. They have less patience for performance.
- She's applied this lens to her marketing work: if she can't explain it to someone in Shillong or Jorhat, she hasn't understood it well enough herself.
- Tier-2 isn't a limitation on her perspective. It is her perspective. And that has made her writing sharper.

**Tone:** Grounded, quietly proud, no chip on shoulder
