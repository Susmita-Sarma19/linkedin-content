# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-03

## Idea 1 — The Parallel Lives

**Angle:** For nine years she held two professional identities simultaneously — live TV host and digital marketer — not as a pivot but as parallel tracks running at the same time. Most career narratives are linear; this one wasn't.

**Hook:** For nine years, I had two jobs. Neither of them knew about the other.

**Outline:**
- What it actually looked like: recording segments for Doordarshan while managing campaigns at Vantage Circle, sometimes in the same week
- How each role quietly fed the other — the broadcast instinct sharpening the marketing eye, the content strategy sharpening the on-camera intent
- The invisible load of holding two professional identities: code-switching not just between languages but between entire modes of showing up
- What she learned about herself from eventually letting one go — and why that felt different from what she expected

**Tone:** Reflective, gently surprising

## Idea 2 — What the Camera Taught the Feed

**Angle:** Specific instincts from nine years of live broadcasting that no digital marketing course covers — and how they changed how she thinks about content strategy.

**Hook:** On live TV, you have about three seconds before an audience decides you're not worth their attention. I didn't learn that in a marketing course.

**Outline:**
- The broadcaster's read: sensing when a room has gone cold before the numbers tell you — and how that instinct translates to spotting when a content calendar has gone flat
- Pacing as strategy — in broadcasting, silence isn't dead air, it's punctuation; in content, most teams are allergic to restraint
- The difference between performing for an audience and writing *to* one — TV forced her to make that distinction early
- One concrete habit she carries from the studio into every content brief she writes today

**Tone:** Earned authority, craft-focused, not nostalgic

## Idea 3 — The Recognition Blind Spot

**Angle:** She builds employee recognition programs for a living. The meta-tension of designing systems for other people's employees while being an employee yourself — and what that contradiction teaches you.

**Hook:** I write about how employees want to feel seen at work. It took me longer than I'd like to admit to ask whether I actually did.

**Outline:**
- The strange position of working in employee engagement: you know all the research, all the frameworks, all the right language — and somehow that knowledge doesn't protect you from the same gaps
- What she noticed about recognition that the frameworks don't capture — the difference between acknowledgment that's designed and acknowledgment that lands
- One thing she started doing differently at work after years of writing about it for others
- The broader point: understanding a thing intellectually and living it are not the same, and that gap is where most workplace culture actually breaks down

**Tone:** Introspective, wry, honest without performing vulnerability
