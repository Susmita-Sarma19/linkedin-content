# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-28

## Idea 1 — The Invisible Audience

**Angle:** Most content advice is built for metros — but Susmita's most engaged readers are from places nobody targets.

**Hook:** Nobody told me people from small towns would be my most loyal readers. I figured it out when I looked at my analytics.

**Outline:**
- The surprise of seeing engagement from Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities — places the content industry rarely writes for
- What it taught her about assumptions baked into "best practices" for digital content
- The irony: being from Guwahati made her write more honestly, which made her more relatable everywhere
- What marketing gets wrong when it only imagines urban audiences

**Tone:** Quietly provocative

## Idea 2 — What Nine Years of Live TV Taught Me About Deadlines

**Angle:** No retakes, no second drafts — TV trained her for the kind of pressure that marketing people say they hate but rarely experience.

**Hook:** When a show goes live, there is no "can we move this to next week."

**Outline:**
- The discipline of live television: you show up, you perform, you have no safety net
- How that muscle — finishing even when it's not perfect — transferred directly into content marketing
- The specific thing most marketers fear that TV cured her of: shipping something imperfect in front of an audience
- Why "done" is a skill, not just an outcome

**Tone:** Punchy, practical

## Idea 3 — The Meeting That Changed How I Think About Recognition

**Angle:** A small, unplanned moment in a team meeting revealed something true about how recognition actually works — and it had nothing to do with a formal program.

**Hook:** It wasn't a rewards ceremony. It was a Tuesday standup. And someone said one sentence that I still think about.

**Outline:**
- The moment: a colleague's small effort got named out loud, specifically, by someone senior — without being asked
- What it felt like in the room versus what recognition platforms promise
- The gap between programmatic appreciation and genuine acknowledgment
- What this means for anyone managing people: specificity > scale

**Tone:** Warm, reflective
