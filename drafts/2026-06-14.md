# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-06-14

## Idea 1 — The No-Undo Classroom

**Angle:** Nine years of live television trained a skill no marketing course teaches — how to recover, not just perform.

**Hook:** For nine years, every mistake I made happened in front of an audience. There was no second take.

**Outline:**
- Live TV runs on presence, not polish — the instinct to recover gracefully, carry on, make it look intentional
- Digital marketing still allows for edits, deletions, "oops wrong link" corrections — live television didn't
- The real skill from the studio wasn't performance. It was composure-under-fire
- What that transfers to content work: done and clear beats perfect and paralyzed

**Tone:** Grounded, slightly wry

## Idea 2 — The Measurement Problem

**Angle:** Employee recognition is deeply human work, but the industry keeps demanding spreadsheet-friendly proof.

**Hook:** At some point, every HR conversation comes back to the same question: but can you measure it?

**Outline:**
- The constant tension in engagement work between qualitative impact and quantitative proof
- Recognition, belonging, psychological safety — real, felt things that resist clean metrics
- The frustration (and occasional comedy) of trying to put a number on "people feel seen at work"
- Where she's landed: measurement matters, but it can't be the only language we use for human experience

**Tone:** Thoughtful, quietly provocative

## Idea 3 — Lost in Translation (But Better For It)

**Angle:** Writing professional content in English while thinking in Assamese and Bengali creates friction — the useful kind.

**Hook:** My first instinct is never in English. The post you're reading right now started in a different language.

**Outline:**
- She thinks in Assamese and Bengali, translates into English for professional writing — not a disadvantage, a filter
- Translation forces precision: if she can't find the English word, she has to ask what she actually means
- Some feelings in Assamese have no clean English equivalent — that gap is where more honest writing lives
- For anyone writing in their second or third language: the translation friction is often where the clarity comes from

**Tone:** Intimate, curious
