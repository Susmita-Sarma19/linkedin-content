# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-10-17

## Idea 1 — The Commerce Degree Nobody Sees Coming

**Angle:** Most HR content communicators arrive through journalism or humanities — she started with commerce and layered communication on top, which gives her a foundation most people in her field build slowly.

**Hook:** My undergraduate degree is in commerce. Not journalism. Not mass communication. Commerce.

**Outline:**
- Most people who know her work assume a humanities background — commerce was the starting point before she moved into mass communication
- Business literacy most communicators pick up slowly on the job (ROI, budget justification, org goals) was her foundation first
- When she writes a business case for employee recognition, she's not translating practitioner language into business language — she knows both, built in opposite sequence
- The "wrong" degree was actually the explanation, not the detour

**Tone:** Quietly reframing

## Idea 2 — What Happens When You Record Instead of Write

**Angle:** Switching from written content to podcasting requires unlearning precision — and that discomfort taught her something about how people actually absorb ideas at work.

**Hook:** The first time I recorded a podcast episode, I said "um" forty-seven times in twenty minutes. I counted.

**Outline:**
- Written content is edited into clarity; spoken content has to be clear while it's happening
- The discipline of live speaking (TV background) doesn't fully transfer — podcast intimacy is different from broadcast presence
- What she noticed: listeners forgive imprecision in a way readers don't, because they're filling gaps with their own experience
- That gap-filling is actually how most learning happens at work — people don't absorb frameworks, they absorb stories with space in them

**Tone:** Curious, specific

## Idea 3 — The Colleague Who Stayed

**Angle:** Six years at one company means she's watched many colleagues leave for "bigger opportunities" — and has had to quietly decide, more than once, that staying was the right call.

**Hook:** I've had the conversation twelve times now. Someone tells me they're leaving. I say the right things. And then I sit with the fact that I didn't.

**Outline:**
- There's a social pressure in the industry to treat staying as settling — the ambitious move is always elsewhere
- Each time a colleague left, she had a version of the same internal audit: is this inertia, or is this choice?
- The honest answer has been different each time — sometimes the answer was proximity to family, sometimes it was genuine belief in what the company was building, sometimes it was both
- What she's learned: having a clear reason you can name to yourself is the difference between staying and being stuck

**Tone:** Honest, introspective
