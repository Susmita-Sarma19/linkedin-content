# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-10-26

## Idea 1 — The Listener's Edge

**Angle:** Nine years of live TV interviews built a specific, transferable skill — listening fast enough to know what question to ask next — that she now applies to content strategy.

**Hook:** "I conducted hundreds of interviews before I understood what I was actually doing. I was never asking. I was listening for what came next."

**Outline:**
- On live television, a bad follow-up question is visible. There's nowhere to hide if you weren't really listening.
- That pressure built a habit: absorb what someone just said before forming the next thought.
- How she applies it now — reading a piece of HR content and asking what the audience is really trying to say, not just what the topic is.
- The best interviews and the best content both start the same way: with genuine attention, not a prepared script.

**Tone:** Craft-focused, earned

## Idea 2 — The Outsider in the Room

**Angle:** She has spent six years writing about HR without ever being in HR — and that distance is not a gap in her credentials, it's the job.

**Hook:** "I've spent six years writing about HR. I've never been in HR. That used to feel like something I should apologize for."

**Outline:**
- The assumption: subject-matter writing should come from people inside the field.
- What she actually brings: the discipline of understanding something from the outside — asking the questions an insider stops asking, noticing what's assumed rather than stated.
- Her background is mass communication and broadcast. She learned to translate complex ideas for general audiences before she learned what employee engagement was.
- The outsider doesn't always know the field. But they often know the audience — because they were once one.

**Tone:** Quietly confident, reframing

## Idea 3 — The Metric We Ignore

**Angle:** Digital marketing obsesses over performance data, but there's an older, harder metric she uses — does the reader feel less alone after reading this?

**Hook:** "My best-performing piece isn't my most-read one. The numbers don't tell you that. You have to know it some other way."

**Outline:**
- Reach and engagement tell you how many people touched the content, not whether it did anything once they got there.
- In six years of HR tech content, the posts that generated the longest conversations weren't the highest-ranking ones.
- The metric she tracks informally: resonance. Does this name something the reader already knew but hadn't said?
- Not an argument against data — an argument for knowing what data can't measure, and not pretending it doesn't matter.

**Tone:** Reflective, practitioner-grounded
