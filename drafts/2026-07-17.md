# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-17

## Idea 1 — The Student on Camera

### Hook
In 2014, I was studying how institutions manage public image.
I was also on camera as the public face of one.
The textbooks were accurate. They were also missing something.

### Angle
From 2014 to 2015, she was enrolled in a PG Diploma in Public Relations and Image Management at Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan in Kolkata — while simultaneously, in Guwahati, she was in her second year as a Talk Show Host at Doordarshan Kendra.

PR theory is clean. It describes how a message is constructed, how institutional perception is shaped, how a visible spokesperson becomes part of the communication architecture. She was reading those frameworks while being one of the instruments through which they operated in practice.

What the diploma gave her wasn't confirmation of the theory. It gave her a vocabulary for the gap between the theory and the studio. The moment the camera is live, the strategic communication framework gives way to something faster — instinct, room, the specific energy of a guest who's had a hard morning. You can't manage an image in real time the way the text describes managing it. You can only stay present enough that the image that forms is honest.

She graduated with the diploma, left for Kolkata, and went into franchise development. The PR framing never left. What it taught her — studied from the outside of a practice she was living from the inside — is that the institutions whose image holds are usually not managing it. They're embodying it.

### Source
PG Diploma in Public Relations/Image Management, Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan's Rajendra Prasad Institute of Communication and Management (2014–2015), pursued while serving as Talk Show Host at Doordarshan Kendra Guwahati (August 2013–December 2022). The PG Diploma credential has not appeared in any prior draft. The specific intellectual experience of studying PR theory while simultaneously being a public-facing institutional voice — what the academic frame clarifies and what it misses — is entirely unexplored. Distinct from all prior angles on the Doordarshan years, which have examined live format discipline, the public mandate, the concurrent digital career, the archive absence, and the pre-broadcast intern phase.

### Resonance
For HR practitioners and internal communicators whose job involves building and protecting organizational culture from the inside: the person who both studies and embodies a practice sees it differently than either the theorist or the pure practitioner. For professionals who have accumulated credentials alongside lived experience: the credential that teaches you most may be the one you were studying while doing something adjacent, because the gap between the framework and the room is where the real learning lives.

## Idea 2 — The Blog Nobody Asked For

### Hook
I have a personal blog I've kept for years.
None of my professional contacts have probably read it.
That's not accidental. That's probably why I've kept it.

### Angle
Six years of writing HR content at Vantage Circle. Thousands of words about recognition programs, engagement surveys, manager behavior — all constructed for a specific reader, a specific problem, a specific commercial context. Every piece has a brief. Every piece has an audience it's meant to reach and a job it's meant to do.

The blog has none of that.

It exists at a different URL, under her name, with no keyword strategy behind it and no product it serves. Nobody assigned it. Nobody tracks it. Nobody will follow up if she stops. She hasn't stopped.

What she's noticed, after years of professional writing with precise intent, is that the blog does something the professional writing can't: it lets her find out what she thinks before she's figured out how to make it useful. The HR content starts from a reader's problem. The blog starts from something she's trying to work out. The two things are both writing, and they require entirely different starting positions.

She's not sure the blog makes her a better professional writer. She suspects it might. What she's certain of is this: the fact that she kept writing it when there was no case for doing so is probably the most honest signal she has about what she actually wants to say.

### Source
susmitype.wordpress.com — Susmita's personal blog, listed on her LinkedIn profile and maintained alongside her professional writing career. No prior draft has examined the coexistence of a personal writing practice and a professional one, or what that split reveals about the distinction between writing as a professional function and writing as a genuine need. The blog URL appears in the profile but the blog itself — what it is, why it exists, what it does that the professional voice can't — has never appeared in any form in the drafts.

### Resonance
For content professionals who write in a professional register all day and maintain some private creative practice alongside it: the two things are not substitutes. They ask for different orientations from the same person. For her HR audience on engagement and intrinsic motivation: the work someone keeps doing when no one is measuring it is the clearest signal of what they actually find meaningful. Recognition systems are built to catch the visible output. The blog nobody asked for is the work that predates the recognition system and will outlast it.

## Idea 3 — The Show as Line Item

### Hook
For two years, my job was deciding which media properties were worth paying for.
I had spent two years being one.
The commercial logic was not what I expected.

### Angle
August 2015. She left Doordarshan temporarily and joined releaseMyAd in Kolkata — an online platform for media buying — as a Media Relations Associate. For two years, she was on the buy side: negotiating placements, evaluating properties, understanding what advertisers were willing to pay and for what.

She had spent the previous two years making a show.

The commercial evaluation of a media property is not what the show feels like from the inside. From the inside, the work is craft and presence and the specific relationship with an audience that forms over years. From the buy side, it's reach, slot, demographic, rate card. The property is a context for a message, valued by how cleanly it delivers the audience the advertiser wanted.

What those two years across the table taught her: the shows that hold their commercial value over time are not the ones optimized for the rate card. They're the ones where the audience relationship is real enough that advertisers want adjacency to it, not just access to the numbers. The craft and the commercial logic aren't opposites. The craft is what makes the commercial case durable.

She came back to Guwahati in January 2018. Then Vantage Circle in 2019. She now produces content for a platform, for an audience of practitioners she's built over six years. The media relations desk gave her a precise language for what that relationship is actually worth.

### Source
Media Relations Associate at releaseMyAd.com (August 2015 – July 2017, 2 years, Kolkata) — a media buying and ad placement platform — taken after her first two years as Talk Show Host at Doordarshan Kendra Guwahati. The Kolkata period at releaseMyAd has appeared only as a timeline marker in prior drafts (The Return, July 12; The Nine Months Nobody Asks About, July 13). None has examined the specific professional experience of the buy-side media role: what it means to evaluate and price media properties commercially when you have been one, and what the commercial frame clarifies about the value of genuine audience relationships over optimized metrics.

### Resonance
For content professionals and digital marketers who think about audience value: the buy side sees what the production side doesn't always articulate — what an audience relationship is worth when it's real versus when it's assembled for a metric. For her HR audience: recognition programs built to optimize the engagement score and recognition cultures that emerge from genuine attention to what people produce both register in the data, but differently, and the difference is visible from the outside before it's measured. The audience can tell whether the relationship is real. So can the advertiser. So can the employee.
