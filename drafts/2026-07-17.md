# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-17

## Idea 1 — The Student on Camera

**Angle:** Studying PR theory while simultaneously being a public-facing institutional voice reveals exactly where the framework breaks down — and that gap is the real education.

**Hook:** In 2014, I was studying how institutions manage public image. I was also on camera as the public face of one. The textbooks were accurate. They were also missing something.

**Outline:**
- The specific cognitive split: enrolled in PR/Image Management theory while actively hosting a live show for Doordarshan
- What academic frameworks clarify vs. what they can't account for — the moment the camera goes live and the theory gives way to instinct
- Institutions whose image holds aren't managing it. They're embodying it.
- The credential that teaches you most is the one you studied while doing something adjacent

**Tone:** Thoughtful, a little wry

## Idea 2 — The Blog Nobody Asked For

**Angle:** Keeping a personal blog for years — separate from all professional work, untracked, with no audience to speak of — is probably the most honest signal about what she actually wants to say.

**Hook:** I have a personal blog I've kept for years. None of my professional contacts have probably read it. That's not accidental. That's probably why I've kept it.

**Outline:**
- Six years of purposeful HR content vs. writing that starts from something she's trying to work out
- What a private writing practice does that professional writing can't — finding out what you think before deciding how to make it useful
- The work someone keeps doing when no one is measuring it is the clearest signal of intrinsic motivation
- Recognition systems catch visible output. The blog nobody asked for predates the system and will outlast it.

**Tone:** Quiet, honest, gently subversive

## Idea 3 — The Show as Line Item

**Angle:** Two years evaluating media properties commercially — after two years of being one — gave her a precise language for what a genuine audience relationship is actually worth.

**Hook:** For two years, my job was deciding which media properties were worth paying for. I had spent two years being one. The commercial logic was not what I expected.

**Outline:**
- The buy-side view: reach, slot, demographic, rate card — a property evaluated for how cleanly it delivers the audience you wanted
- What looks different when you've been on the other side of that calculation
- The shows that hold commercial value over time aren't optimized for the rate card — they're built on a real audience relationship
- The craft and the commercial case aren't opposites. The craft is what makes the case durable.

**Tone:** Precise, with a slow reveal

## Idea 4 — The Translation Test

**Angle:** Trying to say "psychological safety" in Assamese reveals something about the concept itself — when professional jargon can't survive another language, that's not a vocabulary problem.

**Hook:** There's no word for "psychological safety" in Assamese. I've been sitting with that longer than I expected to.

**Outline:**
- The daily experience of working in English, thinking across four languages — the moment a concept stops holding in the second language
- What it means when an HR idea has no equivalent outside its jargon container: not a translation failure, a design failure
- The specific test this creates: if you can't say it plainly in another language, you probably can't say it plainly in English either
- What multilingual practitioners bring to program design that monolingual frameworks miss — they've already stress-tested the assumptions

**Tone:** Intellectually precise, quietly subversive

## Idea 5 — The Tuesday Behind the Data

**Angle:** The shift from writing about employee engagement as content to genuinely caring whether the program works for the actual person on the other end — and when that happened without announcement.

**Hook:** I don't remember exactly when I started caring whether any of this actually changed anything for the person it was supposed to help.

**Outline:**
- Year one: producing accurate content vs. producing content someone could use on a specific Wednesday
- The accumulation that produces genuine investment — not an epiphany, just weight that builds
- What changes in the writing when the abstract metric becomes a real person's workday
- Every engagement score is someone's Tuesday. That's not a metaphor. It's the measure.

**Tone:** Warm, slowly arriving, earnest without performance

## Idea 6 — What Year One Got Wrong

**Angle:** Six years of following HR research produces as many corrected assumptions as confirmed ones — and the thing she believed in 2019 that she understands differently now is more interesting than any lesson.

**Hook:** In 2019, I was certain recognition programs worked because they made people feel valued. Six years later, I think that's approximately true and significantly incomplete.

**Outline:**
- The specific belief she brought into the role: recognition = feeling seen = retention and engagement (the clean causal story)
- What six years of following the evidence actually shows: conditions matter more than the intervention, consistency matters more than size, and the manager is always the variable
- The difference between reading a synthesis at year one and watching how research accretes at year six
- What she's most uncertain about now, that she was most confident about then — and why that reversal is a sign of something working

**Tone:** Rigorous, confident, a little wry
