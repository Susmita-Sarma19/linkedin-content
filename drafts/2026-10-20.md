# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-10-20

## Idea 1 — The Holiday That Doesn't Count

**Angle:** Working in a nationally-distributed company from Guwahati means her biggest festivals don't align with the company's calendar — and quietly navigating that gap has taught her something specific about belonging that her metro colleagues never had to learn.

**Hook:** Durga Puja is the biggest week of the year in my city. On the content calendar, it was labeled "low-traffic window."

**Outline:**
- The calendar tells you, quietly, whose culture the company was built for — not a policy, just the shape of things
- She's navigated this for years without resentment: knowing when to be professionally present and when to be culturally present, and when those two calendars pull in opposite directions
- The invisible labour of self-translation that professionals from non-metro, non-majority contexts do as a matter of course — not because anyone asked them to
- What it means to feel genuinely at home in a workplace when the workplace wasn't quite built with your home in mind

**Tone:** clear-eyed, warm, culturally specific — not a grievance, a testimony

## Idea 2 — Recognition Researcher, Unrecognized

**Angle:** She writes content about why recognition matters to employees. She's also experienced what it feels like when it doesn't happen. The gap between the research and the reality is where the most useful things live.

**Hook:** I've written about employee recognition for six years. I also know exactly what it feels like when it doesn't happen.

**Outline:**
- The particular irony of working inside the recognition industry: knowing all the research doesn't make you immune to the experience
- What she's learned from living on both sides — not as a critic of her company, but as someone who understands the gap between what organisations buy (a platform) and what employees actually need (a habit, a moment, a person who notices)
- The difference between recognition that checks a box and recognition that actually lands — the distinction is almost never about the tool
- The most honest thing she can say about whether the solution she helps market works: it depends entirely on the people running it

**Tone:** honest, quietly ironic, insider-grounded — no axe to grind, just a clear look

## Idea 3 — The Invisible Audience

**Angle:** Nine years of broadcasting to an audience she could never see; six years of writing for an audience she can measure. She's not sure which gave her a truer understanding of who she was actually talking to.

**Hook:** For nine years, I spoke to people I could never see. For six years, I've written for people I can measure. I'm not sure which was more honest.

**Outline:**
- In television, she built a mental image of who was watching and wrote for that person — entirely imagined, entirely necessary
- Digital marketing gave her data: scroll depth, time on page, demographic slices, traffic sources — the audience stopped being imagined and became countable
- What she noticed: the imagined reader she spoke to in TV is often closer to what she actually wants to say than the optimised reader the analytics reveal
- Measurement creates accountability, but it also reshapes what you make — sometimes toward the truth, sometimes away from it; the trick is knowing which direction you're moving

**Tone:** reflective, quietly philosophical, specific — the observation of someone who has worked seriously inside both modes
