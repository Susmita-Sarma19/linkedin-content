# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-05-23

## Idea 1 — The Conference That Doesn't Come Here

**Angle:** On doing national-scale digital marketing work from a city the industry doesn't route its events through — and what that enforced distance taught her about building through output rather than access.

**Hook:** "No one comes to Guwahati to run a content masterclass. I stopped waiting for them to."

**Outline:**
- The unspoken geography of the marketing industry — where the conferences happen, where the panels are, why proximity to that scene is treated as credibility
- What changes when you're not in the room: you either spend energy chasing access, or you build differently
- What she built from here: consistent content, domain authority, a podcast that reaches HR practitioners nationally — none of it required a metro postcode
- The reflection: being far from the scene forced a kind of focus. The work had to speak because the room couldn't

**Tone:** Wry, quietly defiant

## Idea 2 — What TV Taught Me to Unlearn for Podcasting

**Angle:** The specific craft gap between hosting a television show (commanding presence, filling space) and producing a podcast (earning trust through restraint) — and why the skills that made her good at one became the first things she had to question for the other.

**Hook:** "Nine years of television taught me to fill silence. Podcasting took another year to convince me that was wrong."

**Outline:**
- The TV instinct: presence, energy, covering dead air — silence is a problem to solve
- The podcast reality: guests open up in pauses; earbuds are intimate in a way a studio camera isn't; commanding the room is exactly the wrong move
- The specific unlearning: sitting with a pause, letting guests finish thoughts that aren't going anywhere obvious, trusting the edit
- Broader point: every skill has a shadow. The thing that made you effective somewhere can be precisely what you need to question next

**Tone:** Precise, a little self-aware

## Idea 3 — The Difference Between Recognition and Evidence

**Angle:** From inside HR tech, the observable pattern that separates recognition that lands from recognition that reads as performance — it's not about frequency or scale, it's about specificity.

**Hook:** "We talk about employee recognition as if the problem is frequency. Most of the time, the problem is specificity."

**Outline:**
- What hollow recognition looks like in practice: the generic "great job," the blanket badge, the year-end award with no context
- What recognition actually does when it works: it tells someone their specific action was seen and mattered in a particular way — not just that they are valued in general
- The thing six years of writing about recognition taught her: people don't remember the platform, the trophy, or the ceremony — they remember the sentence that proved someone was paying attention
- Reflection: recognition isn't a process problem. It's an attention problem.

**Tone:** Grounded, conviction-led
