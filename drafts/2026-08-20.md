## Idea 1 — The Recognition Ritual

**Angle:** The difference between a company that has a recognition program and one that has a recognition culture isn't the platform — it's the habits that happen before anyone opens the app.

**Hook:** We implemented a recognition program. Usage was fine. Something still felt hollow. It took me two years to understand why.

**Outline:**
- Working in HR tech means watching companies launch recognition tools with full intent and genuine care — and still watching something not quite land
- The program ran. The points were distributed. Managers completed nominations when the quarterly reminder came.
- What was missing: recognition as a reflex, not an event. The difference between noticing something and noting it when prompted.
- The companies where it actually worked had managers who said things out loud — specific things, in the moment, before any system asked them to. The tool just gave that habit somewhere to go.

**Tone:** Precise, earned

## Idea 2 — The Podcast Instinct

**Angle:** What producing HR podcasts taught her that nine years of TV hosting didn't — that the best thing you can do on a mic is ask one question and then genuinely disappear.

**Hook:** I've been on both sides of a microphone. For nine years, the job was to hold the room. Then I started producing podcasts, and I had to learn to let go of it entirely.

**Outline:**
- TV hosting trained a specific instinct: you carry the energy, you fill the silence, you lead — because if you don't, the show drifts
- Podcast production for HR practitioners required something opposite: the guest already knows the thing the listener needs to hear; your job is to reduce friction between those two people
- The moment she understood the difference: an episode where she asked one question and stayed quiet for four minutes while the guest worked something out in real time — and that was the best four minutes of audio she'd produced
- The broader lesson, applied to content work: the most useful thing you can make is sometimes the one that centers someone else's knowledge, not your own

**Tone:** Self-aware, warm, a little surprising

## Idea 3 — What PR Class Actually Taught Me

**Angle:** The PR and Image Management certificate from Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan gave her a frame Mass Communication didn't — and it shapes how she thinks about HR content in ways she's still working out.

**Hook:** In a PR classroom in Guwahati, someone told me: people form an impression of what you've said before they've finished processing whether it's true. I've been writing inside that sentence ever since.

**Outline:**
- Mass Communication gave her the craft: how information moves, how audiences receive it, how media shapes attention
- The PR certification gave her the frame underneath the craft: audiences arrive with a prior, and content either confirms or contradicts it — it doesn't get to override it
- Applied to HR content: an employee who doesn't trust their manager will read a recognition announcement with a different register than one who does; the quality of the announcement changes nothing about that
- What this means for anyone writing internal communications or employee-facing content: you're not just writing a message — you're writing to a cumulative relationship, and the words land differently depending on what's already been deposited there

**Tone:** Intellectual, quietly subversive
