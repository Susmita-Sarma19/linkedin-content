# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-21

## Idea 1 — The Overlap Years

**Angle:** What three years of running two careers simultaneously — TV host and digital marketer — taught about showing up as different versions of yourself without losing the thread between them.

**Hook:** For three years, my workday could start in front of a studio camera and end writing a content brief for an HR newsletter. I didn't have a name for what that was.

**Outline:**
- The specific 2019–2022 overlap: both roles running at once, no one framing it as a "portfolio career" at the time
- What each demanded differently — performance and real-time for one, production and measured output for the other
- The unexpected sharpening: each register kept the other honest; the TV instinct flagged when a content brief got too formal, the content discipline caught when the performance impulse was doing too much
- What closed when the Doordarshan chapter ended — not the skills, but the tension between two modes that had been keeping both cleaner

**Tone:** Reflective, precise

## Idea 2 — The One Who Came Back

**Angle:** Most career advice is about moving toward opportunity. Coming back to Guwahati after Kolkata was a different kind of professional decision — one that took years to understand clearly.

**Hook:** I left Guwahati for Kolkata once. I came back. For a while, I described it as "the right opportunity presented itself." That was partially true.

**Outline:**
- What the move to Kolkata was supposed to be — exposure, scale, a larger professional room
- What being a Northeast person in a metro reveals that you can't see from inside the city you grew up in
- The return as a choice that didn't look like ambition from the outside — and why the outside read didn't match the inside one
- Building a six-year career from a city that wasn't supposed to be the career city, and what that actually did to the work

**Tone:** Quiet, honest

## Idea 3 — What Recognition Can't Measure

**Angle:** Six years of writing about employee recognition professionally sharpens the language for what it is — and makes the gap between the framework and the felt experience impossible to unsee.

**Hook:** I've written hundreds of pieces on employee recognition. I still can't tell you in advance which moment will be the one an employee remembers twenty years later.

**Outline:**
- The professional fluency: what recognition programs measure, what they're designed to do, what good ones accomplish
- The thing the framework describes but doesn't capture — the felt experience of being seen by someone who actually looked
- Why this gap isn't a failure of the program, but the nature of what recognition actually is
- What it means for the content: writing toward that gap rather than writing around it

**Tone:** Measured, honest
