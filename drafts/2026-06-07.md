# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-06-07

## Idea 1 — The Live Edit

**Angle:** Nine years of live television installed a specific reflex — adjust, redirect, recover, without showing the seam — and it turns out that skill runs every campaign she manages, even though the medium looks nothing like a studio.

**Hook:** "Live television doesn't give you a second take. You learn, fast, to edit while the thing is still happening."

**Outline:**
- What the live studio actually trains: not composure in the abstract, but the ability to sense when something has stopped working mid-execution and make a decision before the window closes
- That reflex doesn't retire when you leave the studio — it shows up in how she reads a campaign that isn't landing, how she redirects a content brief that has drifted from what the audience actually needs
- The difference between the planned work and the live work: the plan is the preparation, but the real skill is what you do when the plan meets the actual response
- What she's still learning: the studio version of that reflex is involuntary after long enough; the marketing version requires trusting yourself enough to act on the signal before the data catches up

**Tone:** Precise, grounded, a little surprising

## Idea 2 — The Invisible Table

**Angle:** Podcast production is a strange form of hospitality — she builds the table, prepares the conversation, and never meets the people she made it for. That condition of making for unseen listeners has quietly shaped how she writes everything else.

**Hook:** "I've been producing a podcast for years. I've never met most of the people who listen to it. I think about them anyway."

**Outline:**
- What the studio version of audience was: immediate, visible, responsive — a talk show has a guest in front of you and a crew around you; the feedback is physical and present
- What podcast production removed: all of that, replaced by download numbers and the occasional comment; the listeners are real but entirely invisible
- What that discipline built: a specific practice of imagining the person on the other end before the recording starts — not a persona document, not a demographic, but a specific human in a specific moment who needed to hear this
- That practice runs her HR content now: the manager reading the piece on their commute, the HR lead who has tried everything and is now careful — she writes toward a specific real person, even when she's never met them, because the podcast trained her to

**Tone:** Warm, reflective, quietly specific

## Idea 3 — When You Become the Memory

**Angle:** There is a specific transition in a long tenure that has no ceremony and no official marker: the moment you stop being someone who learns the context and become the person who holds it.

**Hook:** "At some point, I became the person who knows why."

**Outline:**
- The early years: absorbing the institutional context that already exists — why this campaign was tried, why it was changed, what the original brief was before it evolved into something else
- The transition: somewhere in year four or five, the ratio flips; she starts being the person others come to for the context she absorbed without meaning to archive
- What institutional memory actually is: not documentation, not tribal knowledge in the pejorative sense — it's the accumulated map of what the organisation has already tried, what the failures looked like before the current best practice was built
- The thing the resume doesn't capture: the years don't just add up to experience in the general sense; they convert, at some point, into a specific form of operational intelligence that can only be built by staying

**Tone:** Still, quietly authoritative, understated
