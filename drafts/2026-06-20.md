# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-06-20

## Idea 1 — The Attention Economy Lesson

**Angle:** Live TV hosting trained her in audience attention before "content strategy" was even a job title — what that pre-digital instinct looks like in digital work.

**Hook:** I used to have 9 seconds to hold someone's attention before they changed the channel. That habit never left.

**Outline:**
- The pressure of live TV: no edits, no retries, real-time audience feedback with nowhere to hide
- What that trained her to notice — pacing, opening lines, the exact moment a conversation loses the room
- How those instincts show up in digital work: hooks, rhythm, knowing when a post has already lost the thread
- The insight: attention isn't won by volume. It's earned by respecting the audience's time.

**Tone:** Quietly confident, craft-focused

## Idea 2 — The Recognition Paradox

**Angle:** From inside HR tech — the gap between recognition systems that run smoothly and recognition that actually lands.

**Hook:** Most recognition programs fail quietly. Not because they're underfunded — because they're impersonal.

**Outline:**
- What the data in employee engagement says vs. what employees actually tell you when asked directly
- The difference between a system that records appreciation and one that delivers it as something felt
- Why "scalable" and "personal" aren't opposites — but only if you design for the human first, not the process
- What 6+ years in this space taught her: recognition is a communication problem, not an HR operations problem

**Tone:** Substantive, practitioner-voice, gently provocative

## Idea 3 — The Tier-2 Advantage

**Angle:** Working from Guwahati in a metro-dominated industry isn't a gap in context — it's a different, under-represented one worth naming.

**Hook:** Most of my reference points in marketing are from cities I've never lived in. I stopped treating that as a problem.

**Outline:**
- The quiet assumption that "relevant" means metro: the case studies, the conferences, the default examples
- What working outside that default teaches you — including that your audience is probably not in those metros either
- The advantage of not being surrounded by a peer echo chamber that all reads the same content, cites the same names
- The shift: from feeling peripherally located to understanding you hold a perspective most of the industry doesn't

**Tone:** Grounded, a little defiant, warm
