# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-20

## Idea 1 — The Language Your Brain Thinks In

**Angle:** Multilingualism isn't just about reaching different audiences — it's about arriving at the same thought from four different starting positions, and the insights that only travel one route.

**Hook:** I write about employee recognition in English. Some of my best observations about it arrive in Assamese first.

**Outline:**
- Writing HR content in English for 6 years, while living most of her interior life in Bengali and Assamese — the production language and the thinking language aren't always the same
- Each language carries a different relationship to formality, emotional precision, belonging: Bengali for feeling, English for professional authority, Assamese for home and rootedness, Hindi as the bridge in every direction
- Some questions about recognition and workplace belonging are easier to reach in Assamese because the cultural frame is different — not a translation problem, a cognitive starting-position problem
- Multilingualism isn't a communication tool. It's a way of approaching the same question from four different angles, and sometimes only one of them finds the door

**Tone:** quietly observational

## Idea 2 — The HR Content Writer in the Review Room

**Angle:** She spends most of the year writing about how to make performance conversations feel human. Then November comes, and she sits in one.

**Hook:** Every year around this time, the content I've spent twelve months writing comes to find me in a different room.

**Outline:**
- The work: writing about recognition, meaningful feedback, what makes a performance conversation feel like a conversation rather than a verdict — that's the brief for most of the year
- Late November is annual review season. She is also an employee. The desk doesn't change.
- What the gap between the content model and the lived moment actually feels like — not an indictment of either, but the most honest window into where the design falls short and where it doesn't
- The practitioner who goes through the process they write about has a specific kind of access: they know what the room actually feels like when the framework meets the person. That gap is where real HR insight lives, not outside it.

**Tone:** wry and honest

## Idea 3 — Built Without the Room

**Angle:** Most of India's digital marketing culture was shaped in conversations she was never in. Building expertise from that distance produces something different than it removes.

**Hook:** The rooms where most of digital marketing's working assumptions get formed are in Delhi, Bangalore, Mumbai. I have built six years of practice in Guwahati, and I have never been in them.

**Outline:**
- The ambient osmosis of an industry: the post-panel lunch, the informal whiteboard session, the way a consensus forms before it's ever written down — these happen in metro hubs, and geographic distance means you don't absorb them passively
- What replaces it: you become a reader rather than a listener. First-principles frameworks rather than inherited assumptions. You have to build from the actual problem rather than from the current received wisdom about the problem.
- The professional who was never in the casual conversation where the working assumptions got set has a cleaner relationship to received wisdom — freer to question it, more careful about accepting it unreflectively
- Distance from the center isn't a disadvantage with the right workaround. It produces a different kind of expert: one who built the map rather than inherited it.

**Tone:** confident, quietly provocative
