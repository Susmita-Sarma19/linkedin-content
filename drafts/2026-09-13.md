# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-09-13

## Idea 1 — The Art of the Pause

**Angle:** Nine years of live TV taught her that silence is a tool — and that lesson is quietly radical in an industry that treats every content gap as a failure.

**Hook:** I once hosted a guest who paused for ten full seconds before answering. The crew panicked. The audience leaned in.

**Outline:**
- In live broadcasting, silence feels like disaster — but a well-held pause signals that what comes next matters
- In digital marketing, the reflex is to fill every gap: post daily, be everywhere, stay loud
- But some of the most effective content she's seen (and created) comes from choosing *not* to publish — waiting until you have something worth saying
- The discipline isn't silence for its own sake. It's knowing the difference between a pause and an absence.

**Tone:** Reflective, quietly contrarian


## Idea 2 — The Forwarded Email

**Angle:** After six years in HR tech content, she's learned that the work that matters most rarely trends — it gets forwarded to one person who needed to read it.

**Hook:** The best piece of content I ever helped create got 47 views. Someone forwarded it to their manager. That manager changed how they ran their team.

**Outline:**
- The metrics that dominate content strategy — reach, impressions, engagement rate — are real, but incomplete
- HR content exists in a different ecosystem: it often lands in an intranet, a team Slack, a one-on-one conversation
- Measuring success by LinkedIn virality when your actual audience is a people manager at a 200-person company is a category error
- What it looks like to redefine "performing well" when your work is about people, not products

**Tone:** Warm, quietly challenging


## Idea 3 — The Question Behind the Question

**Angle:** The real transferable skill from TV hosting isn't "communication" — it's knowing how to ask the question that gets to the real answer.

**Hook:** Nobody taught me to do audience research. I learned it by interviewing strangers on live television for nine years.

**Outline:**
- On a talk show, the stated topic is rarely what the conversation is actually about — you learn to ask one layer deeper
- That instinct maps directly onto content strategy: what your audience says they want vs. what they actually engage with are often different things
- In HR content specifically, employees rarely articulate what would make them feel more recognised — you have to ask sideways
- The meta-skill she'd put on any content marketer's must-develop list: not research tools, not analytics — the ability to ask the question behind the question

**Tone:** Punchy, specific, slightly unexpected
