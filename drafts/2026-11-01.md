# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-01

## Idea 1 — The November Scramble

**Angle:** Year-end triggers recognition conversations that should have been happening all year — and recognition that arrives on a schedule lands differently than recognition that arrives because someone noticed.

**Hook:** November arrives and suddenly everyone is asking: have we recognised our people this year?

**Outline:**
- The annual review season creates a rush to acknowledge employees before the year closes — bunched, obligatory, arriving at the same time as performance ratings
- What recognition means when it's well-timed vs. when it's calendared: the difference between "I noticed what you did last week" and "it's Q4 and I should say something"
- Employee recognition data shows a spike in formal recognition near year-end — and a flatline most of the rest of the year
- What a recognition culture actually looks like vs. a recognition program: one doesn't need November to remember

**Tone:** Quietly pointed

## Idea 2 — Watching Yourself Work

**Angle:** Nine years of reviewing broadcast footage trained an unusual kind of self-observation — not vanity, but craft — and that training transferred to every medium since.

**Hook:** Every episode at Doordarshan, I watched myself back. Not to cringe. To learn what I'd actually done.

**Outline:**
- What broadcast playback review teaches that no workshop does: the gap between intention and delivery, caught frame by frame
- The specific things you notice watching yourself on tape — the pause that ran too long, the sentence that arrived at the wrong weight — that you never catch from inside the moment
- How that self-observation transferred from camera to writing: she edits her drafts the way she reviewed her footage — looking for where attention would have slipped, not for surface errors
- The difference between ego-driven critique (did I look good?) and craft-driven critique (did it work?) — one gets easier over time; the other never does

**Tone:** Reflective, precise

## Idea 3 — Built in Guwahati

**Angle:** The professional case for building a serious career in a city that doesn't dominate the career narrative — where the noise is lower, the work is more visible, and your relationship to it is different.

**Hook:** No startup scene. No networking dinners. No co-working-space-as-identity. Just the work, and the city I actually know.

**Outline:**
- What it's like to do substantive professional work from outside the metro — and what that quietness makes possible: fewer distractions, a clearer sense of what the work actually is
- The specific texture of professional life in Guwahati: what you have to build yourself (a professional community, a feedback loop, a way to stay current) because the ecosystem won't hand it to you
- How being outside the chatter changes your standards — you can't borrow your identity from the scene, so you have to know what you actually think
- Why she would choose it again — not as a consolation prize, but as a deliberate relationship to the work itself

**Tone:** Grounded, quietly proud
