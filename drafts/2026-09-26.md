# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-09-26

## Idea 1 — The Institutional Memory

**Angle:** Six years at one company isn't just loyalty — it means you carry organizational knowledge that no new hire can shortcut, and that knowledge shows up quietly in every piece of work.

**Hook:** In my sixth year at one company, I started noticing something. I know why things are the way they are. Not from a document. From being there when they weren't.

**Outline:**
- The person who's been somewhere six years holds memory that isn't in any wiki: the failed initiative that sounds like the new one, the product decision that made sense in a context that's since dissolved, the team dynamic that explains the current one
- For marketing work specifically, this shows up as conviction — her campaigns for Vantage Circle aren't abstractly positioned because she knows what the company actually believes, where the language comes from, what sounds authentic versus performed
- The compound understanding that tenure builds isn't just functional expertise; it's a calibrated sense of what the organization is really trying to do, underneath the quarterly plans
- The case for staying: the practitioner who has been inside one place long enough to understand how it thinks is doing something the two-year career-hopper cannot replicate — not because they're more committed, but because they've accumulated something that takes time

**Tone:** Earned, specific — not defensive about staying, just precise about what it builds

## Idea 2 — The Translation Job

**Angle:** The actual work of writing HR content isn't producing articles — it's translating research that lives in academic English into language a manager at 4pm on a Tuesday can act on.

**Hook:** Most of the research my work draws from wasn't written for the person who most needs it. My job is the translation — and translation is nothing like simplification.

**Outline:**
- HR research lives in journals with methodology sections that assume familiarity with survey instruments; the practitioner it's meant to reach is managing a team of eight and reading on a phone
- The craft isn't simplification — simplification loses the nuance that makes a finding useful; translation means finding the vocabulary and frame that carries the substance without hollowing it out
- Six years of this taught her something specific: most HR content fails not because it's poorly researched but because it's translated by someone who never stood in the room the reader stands in — technically accurate, experientially distant
- The best content she's read and the best she's written share one quality: you can feel the writer once had the problem they're writing about

**Tone:** Craft-specific, precise — a practitioner naming what the job actually is

## Idea 3 — What the Report Doesn't Show

**Angle:** Campaign analytics measure what people did; they're structurally silent on who decided not to engage because of something you got wrong — and that silence is where a lot of real learning hides.

**Hook:** My campaigns show me opens, clicks, time on page, conversions. They don't show me the person who read the subject line and decided this wasn't for them. That's also a result.

**Outline:**
- Analytics tell you what happened among people who engaged; they can't show the effect on the reader who decided the brand felt generic, exclusive, or just not built for their situation — that person quietly disappears from the data
- For HR tech content, where the audience immediately pattern-matches against their own workplace experience, getting the tone wrong doesn't produce a bounce rate — it produces silence; the person just doesn't come back
- This is where broadcast training still applies: you couldn't see your television audience either, so you learned to imagine them precisely enough that your instincts stayed calibrated; analytics don't replace that instinct, they supplement it
- The discipline this installed: she writes with awareness of what the dashboard can't see — the trust she's either building or slowly eroding with each piece, in a reader who will never appear in the funnel

**Tone:** Analytical, slightly counterintuitive — a digital marketer thinking from inside the discipline
