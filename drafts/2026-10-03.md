# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-10-03

## Idea 1 — The Attention Economy, Backwards

**Angle:** A former TV presenter's counterintuitive take — she learned to hold attention before engagement metrics existed, and that changes how she sees content now.

**Hook:** Everyone talks about capturing attention. Nobody talks about what to do once you have it.

**Outline:**
- On Doordarshan, you had one broadcast. No algorithm. No re-watch. Viewers stayed because they trusted you.
- TV trained her for continuity over clicks — you built an audience across episodes, not across scroll-stops.
- Digital content often optimises for the hook and loses people three seconds later anyway.
- What actually holds people is consistency and respect for their intelligence — not a better opening line.

**Tone:** Quietly provocative

## Idea 2 — Recognition Isn't a Program

**Angle:** From someone who works inside HR tech — the uncomfortable gap between what companies buy and what employees actually feel.

**Hook:** Most workplaces have a recognition program. Most employees still feel invisible.

**Outline:**
- Recognition platforms are everywhere. Meaningful recognition is still rare.
- The difference between a system and a practice — one you install, one you build.
- What she's seen from years in employee engagement tech: the tool is rarely the problem.
- What actually lands: specificity, timing, and the manager who doesn't wait for a formal occasion.

**Tone:** Direct, practitioner-honest

---

## Idea 3 — The Podcast Host's Secret

**Angle:** What running a podcast taught her about listening — not speaking — and how that changed the way she does everything else.

**Hook:** I've hosted a podcast for years. The most important thing I do on every episode is shut up.

**Outline:**
- Everyone wants to be on a podcast. Few want to prepare the questions.
- The best conversations happen when the host stops performing and starts listening.
- Applied to content work: broadcasting to your audience is not the same as understanding them.
- The discipline of silence — and what it teaches you about who's actually in the room.

**Tone:** Warm, a little wry
