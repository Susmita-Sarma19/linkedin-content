# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-28

## Idea 1 — The Camera-to-Cursor Shift

**Angle:** How 9 years of live TV trained creative instincts that digital marketing still runs on — and what changed when the camera became a cursor.

**Hook:** "On TV, you get one take. On the internet, you get infinite edits. I'm still not sure which is harder."

**Outline:**
- The discipline of live broadcasting — no undo, no second draft, no hiding behind a loading screen
- How that trained a kind of decisiveness: you learn to trust your instincts because hesitation is visible
- What digital work adds that TV never had: data, iteration, audience feedback you can actually measure
- What gets lost in the shift: the live pulse, the room temperature, the human signal that no metric captures

**Tone:** Reflective-wry

## Idea 2 — The Bottleneck Nobody Talks About

**Angle:** The hardest part of employee recognition isn't the budget or the platform — it's getting managers to actually notice.

**Hook:** "We've built more sophisticated recognition systems than ever. The hard part was never the system."

**Outline:**
- What a recognition platform can do and what it genuinely can't — and where that line sits
- The real bottleneck: managers who are stretched too thin to pause and observe
- What "noticing" actually looks like — it's not a ceremony, it's a habit built in ordinary moments
- A workplace where people feel seen is made in small decisions, not annual awards dinners

**Tone:** Direct, practitioner-voice

## Idea 3 — Most Career Advice Isn't Written for Me

**Angle:** The mental map of the Indian professional industry still points to metros — and what it costs when the career advice you find assumes a city you don't live in.

**Hook:** "Most career advice I read is set in a city I don't live in."

**Outline:**
- The assumptions quietly embedded in standard career content: the networking events, the alumni circuits, the "just show up at the office" energy
- What building a digital marketing career from Guwahati actually looks like — different constraints, real advantages, a different pace
- Remote work changed logistics but not the mental map; the industry's imagination still starts in Mumbai or Bengaluru
- What you gain from being outside the noise: market instinct, groundedness, an audience that most metro-focused marketers don't understand

**Tone:** Quietly provocative
