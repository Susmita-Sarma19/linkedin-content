# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-08-25

## Idea 1 — The Meeting That Shaped Me More Than Any Training

**Angle:** The most formative professional development doesn't come from workshops — it comes from watching how someone handles a difficult moment in real time.

**Hook:** I've attended a lot of training sessions. None of them taught me as much as one unplanned conversation I sat in on early in my career.

**Outline:**
- Set the scene: sitting in on a client or team conversation as a junior person, thinking it would be routine
- One person handled something hard — a pushback, a mistake, a tense moment — with unusual calm and clarity
- That moment became a template: what does good actually look like under pressure?
- The lesson: you absorb professional instincts by being in the room, not by reading about them

**Tone:** Reflective, intimate

## Idea 2 — What Nine Years on Camera Taught Me About Writing for Screens

**Angle:** TV hosting and digital content look nothing alike — but the underlying skill is identical: earn attention in the first five seconds, or lose it entirely.

**Hook:** On television, you have about three seconds before a viewer changes the channel. On LinkedIn, it's maybe two sentences. The stakes feel different. The discipline is the same.

**Outline:**
- How live TV trained an instinct for immediacy — no second takes, no editing, the hook has to be right now
- That same instinct applied to writing: the scroll is the remote control
- What changes: medium, format, pacing. What doesn't: the need to make someone feel something quickly
- Why this background is an asset in content marketing that rarely gets named as one

**Tone:** Punchy, unexpected

## Idea 3 — Nobody Talks About What It Costs to Stay

**Angle:** The career conversation is obsessed with when to leave a job — but the quieter, harder question is what it takes to stay and keep growing in one place.

**Hook:** Everyone has advice for when to quit. Almost no one talks about what it costs to stay — and what it gives you if you do.

**Outline:**
- The assumption baked into most career advice: movement equals ambition, staying equals comfort
- What staying actually requires: choosing to grow in place, finding new edges inside a familiar role
- What it gives back: depth over breadth, institutional knowledge, the slow compounding of trust
- Not an argument against leaving — an argument for being intentional about either choice, not reflexive

**Tone:** Quietly provocative, grounded
