# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-09-12

## Idea 1 — The Attention Economy

**Angle:** Nine years of live television taught her something no content course ever will — what it physically feels like to lose a room — and that visceral knowledge is now her sharpest tool in a world of 3-second scrolls.

**Hook:** Live television teaches you something that no content course ever will: what it feels like when you lose a room.

**Outline:**
- On camera, you can see the moment attention slips — a host's instinct is to pull it back, not panic. That calibration lives in the body.
- Digital content gives you no such feedback in real time. So you have to learn to anticipate the drop before it happens.
- The discipline of a 30-second segment and the discipline of a LinkedIn hook are the same problem at different speeds.
- Nobody lists "9 years of live TV" as a content marketing credential. They should.

**Tone:** Punchy, insider

## Idea 2 — The Recognition Paradox

**Angle:** The strange position of spending years writing content about employee recognition — what it does, why it matters, how to do it right — while also being an employee who sometimes felt invisible at work.

**Hook:** I've written more about employee recognition than most people will read in a lifetime. I've also felt invisible at work. Both things are true.

**Outline:**
- There's a particular kind of dissonance in crafting case studies about recognition cultures while sitting inside your own experience of being seen or not seen.
- It doesn't make the work less meaningful — if anything, the personal stakes make it sharper.
- What working in HR tech taught her: recognition isn't a program. It's a decision someone makes about whether another person's effort counts.
- The best content she's written on this came from the hardest moments, not the easiest ones.

**Tone:** Reflective, quietly honest

## Idea 3 — The Trend Watcher

**Angle:** Having stayed at one company for 6+ years while working in HR content, she's had a front-row seat to every major workplace trend cycle — and what she's noticed is that the trends change, but the underlying human need never does.

**Hook:** Quiet quitting. The Great Resignation. Bare minimum Mondays. I watched all of it from the same desk.

**Outline:**
- Every year in HR tech brings a new term for the same thing: people trying to tell their employers something isn't working.
- The terminology cycles. The LinkedIn discourse spikes and fades. The actual problem — people feeling disconnected from their work — doesn't go anywhere.
- Staying at one company long enough means you stop chasing the trend and start watching the pattern.
- What 6 years in this space has taught her: the language of work keeps changing. The need to feel like your work matters doesn't.

**Tone:** Wry, grounded
