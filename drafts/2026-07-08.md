# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-08

## Idea 1 — The Audience Instinct

**Angle:** Nine years of live television taught her something no analytics dashboard can — how to feel when you've lost your audience, and that instinct travels into digital work.

**Hook:** "I used to know when an interview was dying. Not from the data. From the room."

**Outline:**
- Live TV has no edit button and no real-time metrics — you read the audience or you lose them, and you find out immediately
- That instinct translated: knowing mid-paragraph when a LinkedIn post loses a reader, when a campaign is technically correct but emotionally flat
- Most digital marketers learn to read audiences through dashboards; she learned on a studio floor with a camera pointed at her face
- The metric you can't measure is still a metric — and ignoring it is its own choice

**Tone:** Sharp, slightly wry

## Idea 2 — The Tier-2 Tax

**Angle:** Building a career from Guwahati means no industry events at your doorstep, no accidental hallway introductions — and what fills that gap turns out to be more durable than proximity.

**Hook:** "Every 'networking event' I've attended in the last six years, I had to fly to."

**Outline:**
- The informal economy of career growth — bumping into the right person, being in the room when the opportunity surfaces — doesn't work the same way from a Tier-2 city
- What you build instead: deliberate relationships, online presence, work that can travel without you
- The discipline it creates: you can't coast on proximity, so the work has to speak clearly enough on its own
- Not a complaint — an observation the industry rarely stops to account for

**Tone:** Dry, precise, quietly pointed

## Idea 3 — The Recognition Paradox

**Angle:** She writes about employee recognition for a living — which has given her an unusually specific lens on the gap between what we design for employees and what we casually withhold from the people closest to us.

**Hook:** "I spend my days writing about employee recognition. I still remember the one time a manager noticed something small I'd done — offhandedly, months after the fact."

**Outline:**
- Working in HR tech means knowing the research cold: recognition is one of the highest-impact levers for engagement, and its absence quietly erodes things
- But knowing the theory and feeling the moment are two entirely different experiences
- The people who build recognition systems professionally aren't immune to needing it themselves — and that's not irony, it's just human
- A small, honest observation: understanding something deeply doesn't mean you practice it perfectly at the level closest to you

**Tone:** Reflective, quietly tender
