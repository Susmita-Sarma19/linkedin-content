# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-09-01

## Idea 1 — The Invisible Audience

**Angle:** Whether you're hosting a TV show or writing content for an HR app, you're always talking to someone who isn't in the room — and most people forget this.

**Hook:** I've been talking to people I can't see for fifteen years. First on television. Now on screen.

**Outline:**
- TV hosting trained her to imagine one specific viewer — not a crowd, not a demographic
- Digital marketing asked for the same skill: the employee reading a recognition notification never meets you
- The mistake most brand content makes: it speaks to the company, not the person
- What actually changes when you write to one invisible human instead of a target audience

**Tone:** Reflective, quietly revealing

## Idea 2 — Recognition as Communication

**Angle:** Most employee recognition programs don't fail because companies don't care — they fail because appreciation is treated as an event, not a conversation.

**Hook:** Your company probably has a recognition program. Be honest: does it feel like anyone means it?

**Outline:**
- The gap between what recognition dashboards show and what employees actually carry home
- Recognition as a one-way broadcast vs. something that lands like it was said by a person
- What HR tech does well (visibility, frequency) and where it still falls short (voice, specificity)
- The question that reframes everything: "Would you say this to their face?"

**Tone:** Pointed, expert

## Idea 3 — The Guwahati Confidence

**Angle:** There's a particular kind of confidence required when you're building an industry presence from a city that doesn't appear on the industry map.

**Hook:** The first time I joined a national marketing conference call, I didn't mention I was calling from Guwahati.

**Outline:**
- The quiet calculation of geography: when to lead with your city, when not to
- The moment she stopped treating her location as something to explain or apologize for
- What "far from the center" actually gave her — an outsider's clarity that metro practitioners often don't have
- Building credibility without a metro address: slower, and in some ways more durable

**Tone:** Personal, quietly defiant
