# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-27

## Idea 1 — What Camera-Ready Actually Means

**Angle:** Nine years of live TV changes what "showing up" means — a perspective that reframes the digital content pressure everyone suddenly feels.

**Hook:** When you've done live television, the phrase "just be yourself on camera" lands differently.

**Outline:**
- Doordarshan was live — no second takes, no editing yourself into someone more polished. You learned to be present, not perfect.
- The current obsession with "camera-ready" content (reels, LinkedIn videos, podcast clips) often chases polish over presence
- What TV actually taught: the audience doesn't want flawless. They want real. They can tell the difference in seconds.
- Closing reflection: Presence is a skill, not a setting. You can't download it. You practice it.

**Tone:** Quietly authoritative


## Idea 2 — The Anti-Niche

**Angle:** A pushback on "niche down" advice, from someone whose career only makes sense as an intersection — and whose value comes precisely from that.

**Hook:** Every piece of career advice I've read tells me to pick one thing. I never did. I'm not sure I was wrong.

**Outline:**
- The niche-down gospel assumes depth beats breadth. But some work requires both: HR content that actually reaches people, marketing that understands what employees feel, podcasting that knows how to hold attention
- When your background is TV + marketing + HR tech + multilingualism, the "niche" is the intersection itself
- The risk isn't being too broad. The risk is not knowing what thread runs through it all. (For her: people. Making work feel human.)
- Not an argument against focus — an argument against false narrowing

**Tone:** Gently provocative


## Idea 3 — The Story Companies Tell About Their People (And Why It Doesn't Land)

**Angle:** From inside HR tech content, a frank look at the gap between what companies think makes a good employee story and what employees actually recognize as true.

**Hook:** Companies love telling employee stories. Employees rarely believe them.

**Outline:**
- Working in HR content means seeing a lot of "meet our people" campaigns — the ones with stock-photo smiles and sentences that sound like job descriptions with feelings added
- What actually resonates: specificity, contradiction, something that could only have been said by that person
- The pattern that fails: the story edited until it becomes an ad. No rough edges, no real voice.
- What it costs when people read their own story and don't recognize themselves in it — the quiet credibility damage that's hard to measure but very real

**Tone:** Insider, candid
