# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-09-14

---

## Idea 1 — The Second Job Nobody Sees

**Angle:** For nearly a decade, she ran two full professional lives simultaneously — TV host and digital marketer — and most people on LinkedIn know only one of them.

**Hook:** For nine years, I had two jobs. Not a side hustle. Two complete careers, running in parallel, in different industries, under different names.

**Outline:**
- The math: Doordarshan (Aug 2013–Dec 2022) overlapped with the start of her digital marketing work at Vantage Circle by several years; neither employer fully knew the texture of the other
- What running two professional identities actually required — code-switching between live broadcast and content dashboards, between a studio floor and a laptop at 11pm, between a camera and a blank document
- The invisible integration: how being a content strategist made her a better interviewer, how interviewing made her a better brief-writer; the skills that migrated below the surface
- When Doordarshan ended in December 2022, only one identity remained visible on paper — what it means to carry a decade of craft that your LinkedIn profile doesn't quite capture

**Tone:** Honest, unguarded

## Idea 2 — The Reader at 11pm

**Angle:** HR content isn't read in comfortable, reflective conditions — it's reached for urgently, by practitioners facing hard conversations tomorrow morning.

**Hook:** I've spent six years writing about employee recognition. I used to think about what I was saying. Now I mostly think about who's reading it, and when.

**Outline:**
- Most content metrics tell you how many people arrived; they don't tell you what was at stake when they did — the HR manager the night before a performance review, the team lead reading about manager feedback after a meeting that went wrong
- The specificity of HR as an audience: they are not consuming information casually; the content is often preparation for something difficult and human
- How that changes the job: the question isn't "is this accurate?" but "does this hold when the stakes are real?" Writing for someone who will use it in the room, not just remember it
- The quiet responsibility of producing content for practitioners whose work directly shapes how other people experience their jobs — and why that's not a weight she wants to put down

**Tone:** Warm, quietly serious

## Idea 3 — The City You Didn't Leave

**Angle:** Choosing to build a career in Guwahati when the industry's gravity pulled toward metros — not as sacrifice, not as limitation, but as a deliberate decision with real trade-offs in both directions.

**Hook:** I have never worked in Delhi, Mumbai, or Bangalore. In my industry, that's still a sentence that requires explanation.

**Outline:**
- The specific professional pressure that points toward metros: where the conferences are, where the headquarters are, where the networking happens, where the salary bands sit
- What staying in Guwahati actually cost and what it didn't — the honest version, not the inspirational one; the opportunities that didn't arrive, and the ones that arrived because of where she was
- The Northeast as professional context: what it means to work in HR tech for a company with a Guwahati team, producing content for a global audience, from a city that most of that audience has never thought about
- The thing she couldn't have built from a metro: a specific kind of double vision — knowing what national industries look like from the inside, and knowing what they look like from a place those industries tend to treat as a market segment rather than a home

**Tone:** Grounded, unsentimental
