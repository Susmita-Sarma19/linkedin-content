# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-09

## Idea 1 — The Image Maker's Blind Spot

**Angle:** A postgrad in PR and Image Management is supposed to teach you to shape perception — but the harder lesson is understanding the gap between the image you project and the person doing the projecting.

**Hook:** I have a postgrad degree in Image Management. For years, I thought that meant I understood how to manage an image. It took longer to understand that managing your image and being yourself are almost always working against each other — and that knowing which one to choose, and when, is the real skill.

**Outline:**
- What image management actually teaches (how perception is built) vs. what it implies (that the right image is worth building deliberately)
- Nine years on camera as a talk show host exposed the gap between performance and presence — she learned to hold them simultaneously, and to notice when others can't
- In digital marketing and HR content, that gap shows up constantly: brands that project values they don't hold, recognition programs that perform care without practicing it
- The credential was useful; the gap it opened up was more useful

**Tone:** searching, honest

## Idea 2 — What Thirteen Years With a Microphone Taught Me About Not Talking

**Angle:** The defining skill of a good host isn't having good questions — it's knowing when to stay quiet, and most workplaces have never learned this.

**Hook:** I've spent roughly thirteen years in a room with a microphone, waiting for someone to finish their sentence. It has made me very good at noticing when no one is actually listening.

**Outline:**
- The specific discipline of hosting: tracking what a guest circles back to, what they avoid, what they say when they think the important part is already over
- The difference between performing listening (nodding, summarizing) and actually listening (staying in uncertainty long enough to hear something you didn't expect)
- How this maps to HR's most consistent failure: organizations design mechanisms for employee voice and then don't know what to do with what they hear
- What it actually looks like to be in a room where someone is genuinely listening — it's rare enough that people remember it

**Tone:** calm, quietly pointed

## Idea 3 — The Credential That Doesn't Fit a Box

**Angle:** Four languages don't appear on a resume the way a degree does, but being able to hold four linguistic registers has shaped her content instincts more than any formal qualification.

**Hook:** I listed four languages on my CV for years before I understood what actually knowing four languages gives you. It's not translation. It's the ability to feel the gap between what you said and what your reader understood — and to care about closing it.

**Outline:**
- What fluency across languages teaches: you can't assume what your reader already knows, because every language carries a different set of assumptions about what's shared
- The professional invisibility of this — multilingualism doesn't map onto a credential, so it rarely gets counted as expertise, even when it's doing significant work
- In HR content specifically, the gap between what organizations say and what employees understand is the central problem; she came to that problem with a practitioner's feel for it, not a theory
- What it means to write for an audience you can actually see, because you've had to earn that visibility across four different registers

**Tone:** quietly proud, specific
