# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-02

## Idea 1 — The Content Without a Name

**Angle:** Six years of making organizational content that carries the company's name, not hers — and what the absence of attribution teaches about why you actually do creative work.

**Hook:** Most of what I've written at work, you'll never know I wrote.

**Outline:**
- What "organizational content" actually is — the guides, articles, recognition frameworks that HR managers read and use, published under a company banner with no byline
- The specific discipline of making something carefully when your name is never attached to it
- The irony she sits inside: working in employee recognition technology while creating entirely unattributed work
- What you discover about your own motivation when personal credit is structurally removed — whether you're writing for the work, or for the record of it

**Tone:** Quietly honest

## Idea 2 — The Sixty Seconds Before Live

**Angle:** What nine years of live television taught about the internal state before stepping into something you can't take back — and how that composure translates to professional moments that have nothing to do with cameras.

**Hook:** There are sixty seconds before the camera goes live. I stood in them hundreds of times. What happens in them has nothing to do with confidence.

**Outline:**
- What those sixty seconds actually are — not rehearsal, not final prep, not the absence of nerves. A specific state she learned to recognize and use.
- The irreversibility of live broadcast: once the signal goes out, there is no revision, no recall. What that constraint does to how you prepare before you ever get to those sixty seconds.
- How the same internal state shows up far from cameras — before a high-stakes presentation, before sending something important, before a difficult conversation
- The thing broadcasting clarified: composure isn't calm. It's knowing what to do with everything you feel when you can no longer change what's about to happen.

**Tone:** Still, precise, unexpectedly personal

## Idea 3 — The Reader Who Sees Through It

**Angle:** Writing HR content for HR professionals — an audience trained in communication, influence, and behavior — means writing for people who are fluent in every rhetorical move you might use. What that constraint demands from the writing.

**Hook:** My reader is, professionally speaking, harder to fool than most.

**Outline:**
- HR professionals have done the communication workshops, read the persuasion research, run the engagement surveys. They know what content is trying to do to them before they've finished the first paragraph.
- What it means to write for an audience that sees through the architecture of your argument — you can't lead them to a conclusion they didn't already sense was coming
- The discipline this forces: you have to actually have something to say, not just a frame for saying it. The sophisticated reader strips the scaffolding fast.
- What six years of writing for this particular reader taught about the difference between content that sounds like it says something and content that does

**Tone:** Sharp, a little wry, self-aware
