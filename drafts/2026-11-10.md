# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-10

## Idea 1 — The Camera Before Zoom Existed

**Angle:** Years of live TV hosting built a kind of on-camera discipline that most professionals are still scrambling to learn now that video calls are the default.

**Hook:** "Everyone wants to 'seem natural' on camera. I had a decade of live TV to understand why that's the wrong goal."

**Outline:**
- Live hosting taught that authentic presence isn't natural — it's a practiced, deliberate thing
- Managing dead air, nerves, and an audience you can't see are skills that transfer directly to webinars, all-hands calls, and virtual presentations
- The difference between reading a script and actually being in the room with someone
- What most people never get to practice because they started their professional lives on Zoom

**Tone:** Warm, knowing — the voice of someone who's figured something out the long way and isn't smug about it


## Idea 2 — Who Is HR Content Actually For?

**Angle:** Most employee engagement content is written from the manager's vantage point — which means it often misses the person it's supposedly about.

**Hook:** "Most HR content talks about employees as if they're a gap to be closed, not people to be listened to."

**Outline:**
- The quiet assumption baked into a lot of workplace content: the reader is the person with the solution, not the one with the experience
- What it means to write about recognition for a living while also being someone who needs to feel recognized
- How that double perspective — insider and practitioner — changes what you're willing to write
- The question every HR communicator should sit with before they hit publish: who is this actually for?

**Tone:** Quietly provocative — a professional asking a question she doesn't get asked enough


## Idea 3 — What Mass Communication Teaches That Marketing Degrees Don't

**Angle:** A Mass Comm background is an underrated foundation for content and marketing work — but it rarely shows up in job descriptions, so practitioners with it have to translate.

**Hook:** "My degree is in Mass Communication. That still needs explaining in some rooms."

**Outline:**
- What Mass Comm actually trains you in: audience theory, message construction, thinking in terms of medium and context — not just output
- How that differs from a conventional marketing education, and why both have blind spots
- The skills it gave her that she uses every day but that hiring processes don't know how to ask for
- A case for non-traditional paths into content work — and for being generous about what counts as relevant experience

**Tone:** Grounded, confident — not defensive, just clear
