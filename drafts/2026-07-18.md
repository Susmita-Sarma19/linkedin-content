# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-18

## Idea 1 — The Six-Week Window

### Hook
My first two months at Doordarshan, I was a production intern — making sure other people were ready to go on camera. Six weeks later, I was in front of it. Nobody narrated that transition. There wasn't time.

### Angle
June 2013: production intern at Doordarshan Kendra Guwahati. Setting up the shoot, running the logistics, learning the studio from the side that makes the talent possible. By August 2013 — same institution, same floors, much of the same crew — she was the Talk Show Host.

The production side teaches you something the talent-facing side can't: what the camera is actually doing, what holds before the moment, what the technical conditions demand of the person standing in front of them. She knew the shot before she was in it. She understood the segment from the perspective of the people making it run.

What the internship didn't prepare her for: how quickly readiness would be decided. Most professional frameworks suggest a longer distance between learning a craft and being asked to perform it. Two months is not that distance. She didn't feel ready in the way training implies readiness. But the opportunity was there, and she took it, and she found the preparation she needed inside the doing rather than before it.

The institution's confidence was faster than her own. She's thought about that since.

### Source
Production Intern, June–July 2013, Doordarshan Kendra Guwahati; Talk Show Host, August 2013–December 2022, same institution. The two-month internship followed immediately by a hosting role in the same place — the speed of that transition, the production-side knowledge it granted, and what it reveals about readiness and institutional confidence — has never appeared in any prior draft.

### Resonance
For professionals who have been asked to step into a role before feeling ready: the institution often makes the confidence call before you do. For HR practitioners thinking about promotion readiness and talent development: "ready" is frequently an internal experience that lags behind actual capability — and the person who has spent time watching the job from the inside may be better prepared than the formal framework suggests, even before the framework says so.

## Idea 2 — The Franchise Chapter

### Hook
For five months in 2015, I helped expand a fast food franchise into Northeast India.
It's the one role on my resume that connects least to everything that came after.
It's also the one that made clearest what I actually needed from work.

### Angle
March to July 2015. Subway Systems India. The development office covering North East and Eastern India, based in Kolkata. The work: franchise coordination, regional expansion. Not media, not HR, not content. An operational role in a global consumer brand she hadn't worked toward and wouldn't build on.

She'd come from Doordarshan — public-facing, editorial, connected to something she cared about making. She'd go on to releaseMyAd, back into media. The five months in between were different in a precise way: the pull wasn't there. She could do the work. The work wasn't wrong. But nothing about it produced the kind of investment she'd had in the craft before or the one that came after.

That's the information a detour gives you that a deliberate move can't. When you're building toward something, you don't know how much the pull is doing. You only notice when it's gone. Five months of competent, motivated-enough, functionally fine work made clear what the years at Doordarshan had actually been — not just a job she was good at, but work that engaged something past the task.

She left in July 2015. Not because the franchise role was bad. Because she'd learned what she needed from the contrast.

### Source
Coordinator, Subway Systems India Pvt. Ltd, March–July 2015, Kolkata — the development office for Northeast and Eastern India. This biographical detail is entirely unexplored in any prior draft. It sits between her time at Doordarshan and her move to releaseMyAd, and is the role in her career history least obviously connected to the trajectory she built, making it a precise lens on what she actually values in her work.

### Resonance
For professionals who've had a role that didn't fit the story they were building, and who understand in retrospect that the mismatch was information rather than failure. For her HR audience on engagement and retention: a person can be competent and motivated-enough without being genuinely invested. The five-month signal is often more reliable than the first-month impression. What you notice when someone who was engaged elsewhere goes quiet is the same thing she was learning about herself — from inside.

## Idea 3 — Both Sides of the Signal

### Hook
In September 2013, I spent a month in rural Guwahati asking people whether they were watching Doordarshan.
I was already a Talk Show Host on Doordarshan.

### Angle
A research campaign on the reach and access of Bharat Nirman advertisements, under IIMC New Delhi. Field survey work. Rural communities around Guwahati. The methodology: go out, ask people whether the government's broadcasting is reaching them, whether they're actually seeing what the institution is producing.

She was the institution's host. She was also the person in the field asking whether the institution was reaching anyone.

What she found: the gap between "we broadcast it" and "it arrived" was real and measurable and not what the production side assumed. Some of what Doordarshan produced was landing differently than the channel understood. Some reached the intended audience. Some did not. The reasons were specific — reception infrastructure, timing, relevance to the questions those communities were actually carrying.

She's been asking the same question ever since. Not in villages, but in content. Not about government advertising, but about HR articles — whether what she's made for the practitioner who searches for it is actually reaching the person who needed it, in the situation they were in. The question is structurally identical. The medium changed.

The field work in September 2013 was the first version. She didn't know she'd still be asking it a decade later.

### Source
Surveyor, Research Campaign on Reach and Access of Bharat Nirman Advertisements, under IIMC New Delhi, September 2013, rural Guwahati area — concurrent with the start of her role as Talk Show Host at Doordarshan Kendra Guwahati (August 2013–December 2022). The specific position of being both a broadcaster and a researcher measuring whether a broadcaster's content reached its intended audience — in the same month, at the same institution — has never appeared in any prior draft. This is the biographical origin of a question she has returned to across her entire career, in very different contexts.

### Resonance
For content professionals, HR practitioners, and internal communicators who have ever wondered whether the thing they made reached the person they made it for: the gap between production and reception is almost always larger than the producing institution assumes. The post names the origin of that question for her, and traces it forward. For her HR audience specifically: recognition programs, engagement surveys, and L&D content carry the same structural challenge the village research captured. Did it actually get there? That's still the question.
