# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-18

## Idea 1 — The Invisible Audience

**Angle:** Writing HR content for six years means writing for practitioners who apply your ideas in meeting rooms and never tell you — and learning to find gratification in impact you can't measure.

**Hook:** Most of the people who've used my work have never left a comment. Most of them never will. It took me a while to genuinely be okay with that.

**Outline:**
- Writing for HR practitioners is structurally different from writing for engagement: the return isn't in likes, it's in whether a manager actually changed how they run a 1:1 on Monday
- What this does to your relationship with metrics when your best impact is invisible — and how you stop chasing the wrong signal
- The specific shift: realizing the goal isn't a post that performs, it's a framework that gets used in a room you'll never see
- What "content that works" actually means in practitioner-facing writing, and why most content strategy advice doesn't account for it

**Tone:** Quietly subversive, precise

## Idea 2 — What Recognition Taught Me About Being Recognized

**Angle:** Six years of writing about employee recognition revealed more about her own relationship with being seen at work than any performance conversation ever did.

**Hook:** I've written more words about employee recognition than most people will read on the subject in their careers. I'm still working out what it actually feels like to receive it.

**Outline:**
- The specific gap: knowing the research (specificity matters, timing matters, sincerity matters) and navigating your own need for acknowledgment are two different muscles
- What happens when you're the subject-matter expert on something you're also experiencing — the knowledge doesn't make it easier, it makes you more precise about what's missing
- Getting promoted last year changed the frame: being on the receiving end of her own team's expectations surfaced this differently
- Not a resolution — a quiet observation about the distance between knowing something and living it

**Tone:** Honest, a little wry, unsentimental

## Idea 3 — The City That's Also Building Itself

**Angle:** She built her career in Guwahati across a decade when the city itself was mid-transformation — and the parallels aren't incidental.

**Hook:** When I started at Doordarshan in 2013, Guwahati didn't have a metro rail. Neither did my career have a clear direction. Both are different now. Not finished — different.

**Outline:**
- Guwahati's specific trajectory: not a generic Tier-2 narrative, but a real place — airport expansions, infrastructure investment, the specific industries arriving and why
- What it means to build professional identity in a city that's also building itself, not a finished backdrop — the place is part of the story, not the reason the story is smaller
- Working in HR tech for a global product audience from this city: what Guwahati taught her about what belonging and engagement actually mean, beyond the framework
- The Northeast is not homogenous. Guwahati is a specific place, changing in specific ways. That specificity is the point.

**Tone:** Rooted, observational, quietly proud
