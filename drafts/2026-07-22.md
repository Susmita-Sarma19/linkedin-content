# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-07-22

## Idea 1 — The Invisible Collaborator

**Angle:** Six years of writing for HR practitioners without ever being one — and what that gap forced her to do that expertise never would.

**Hook:** I've spent six years writing about employee recognition. I have never once been responsible for running an employee recognition program.

**Outline:**
- The strange authority that comes from being a dedicated observer, not a practitioner — you listen differently when you can't assume you already know
- What HR professionals actually need from content vs. what sounds credible from the outside
- How the gap forced a discipline: every claim had to be earned through listening, not through lived experience
- Why "informed outsider" sometimes catches what practitioners, too close to the work, stop noticing

**Tone:** Honest, quietly subversive

## Idea 2 — The Second Question

**Angle:** Most recognition programs measure what they sent, not whether it landed — and that distinction, borrowed from media fieldwork, is where the real design problem lives.

**Hook:** "We ran 2,000 recognition moments last quarter." Okay. Did any of them matter to the person on the other end?

**Outline:**
- The metric that's easy to count (recognitions sent, programs run) vs. the one that's harder but more important (did the employee actually feel seen?)
- How organizations default to output data because outcome data asks an uncomfortable question
- A lesson from her early research on broadcast reception: transmission is not communication, and publication is not reception
- What it would look like to design recognition programs around the second question, not the first

**Tone:** Sharp, diagnostic

## Idea 3 — The Accent in the Room

**Angle:** There was a phase where she worked to sound less like she was from Guwahati. The day she stopped was when her professional voice actually started.

**Hook:** There was a phase where I worked very hard to sound less like I was from Guwahati. I'm glad I stopped.

**Outline:**
- The quiet pressure on Northeast professionals to neutralize their accent in formal or metro-facing settings
- What gets stripped away when you iron out the regional texture — not just sound, but perspective and specificity
- What nine years on Doordarshan actually taught her: cadence is not a liability, it's a tool; presence is built, not borrowed
- The difference between a voice that sounds like the default and a voice that's yours — and why the second one is harder to dismiss

**Tone:** Personal, steady, quietly defiant
