# LinkedIn Ideas — 2026-11-24

## Idea 1 — The Audience of One

**Angle:** Nine years of TV with no analytics dashboard taught her a different kind of audience awareness — one built on direct, human contact, not data. The move to metrics-driven digital content changed not just how she measures reach, but what she understands connection to mean.

**Hook:** For nine years, I had no idea how many people were watching. The ones who reached me did it in person.

**Outline:**
- Regional broadcast had no impressions counter — audience response came through studio calls, letters, and chance encounters in ordinary places
- A specific: a viewer who stopped her at a vegetable market to mention last week's episode, quietly, the way people mention things that actually got through
- The intimacy of unquantified attention — you hear from the person who was moved, not the person who scrolled past
- Digital content life: reach, CTR, engagement rate — a different vocabulary for a different kind of knowing
- What she notices now that metrics can't tell her: whether anything crossed, as opposed to whether anyone saw it

**Tone:** Reflective, quietly unsettled

## Idea 2 — What Doesn't Scale

**Angle:** She writes about employee recognition for a platform built on scalability. The recognition she's seen actually matter has never been scalable. Holding that contradiction inside the work is not a problem to solve — it's what keeps the work honest.

**Hook:** Every recognition program I've written about is built to work at scale. The ones I've seen actually change something never did.

**Outline:**
- Recognition technology optimizes for repeatability: same process, every employee, predictable cadence
- The recognition moments that shifted something were specific, context-dependent, unrepeatable — a manager who noticed the unglamorous thing the team never celebrated
- The gap between recognition that processes everyone fairly and recognition that sees one person specifically
- Six years of writing about this: not trying to resolve the tension, but refusing to pretend it isn't there
- What HR content that holds this contradiction reads like, as opposed to content that smooths it over

**Tone:** Honest, methodical, a little sharp

## Idea 3 — The Invisible Record

**Angle:** Six-plus years at one company means her professional growth generates no external signal — no string of job changes, no announcement posts. What it looks like to build a career that professional platforms aren't designed to represent.

**Hook:** My LinkedIn profile has had one company in "Experience" for six years. It makes my career look simple. It isn't.

**Outline:**
- Professional platforms are optimized to track movement — each new role a data point, a legible signal of growth and ambition
- Internal growth doesn't produce those signals: expanded scope, deeper expertise, new responsibilities all happen without a notification or announcement
- What six years at one company has produced that a sequence of two-year stints couldn't — depth of institutional knowledge, real consequence for work, relationships that survive a bad quarter
- The person who stayed isn't the person who didn't move; they're the person whose movement didn't show up as a job change
- Not an argument against switching — an observation about what visibility systems miss

**Tone:** Dry, quietly confident, slightly pointed
