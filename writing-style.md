## My Writing Tone
- **Conversational-professional** — not stiff, not casual. Think: smart colleague, not corporate memo.
- Clarity over cleverness. Say it plainly, say it well.
- Practical and actionable — readers should walk away knowing something or feeling something.
- No jargon for the sake of it.

## What I Write About
- Employee engagement, recognition, and workplace culture
- HR content for practitioners and managers
- Digital marketing and content strategy
- Personal career reflections and professional growth

## My Voice Pillars

### 1. Grounded
I don't chase trends. My perspective comes from real experience — years in media, years in HR tech, years in a city that doesn't always get a seat at the table. That groundedness shows in how I write.

### 2. Human-first
People spend most of their lives at work. That belief shapes everything I write. I'm not writing about processes — I'm writing about people.

### 3. Quietly confident
I don't shout. I don't oversell. I make my point clearly and let it stand. The TV background gave me stage presence; the writing work gave me discipline.

### 4. Culturally aware
Four languages, one Northeast perspective in a metro-dominated industry. I write with an awareness that audiences are not monolithic. Context matters. Who you're speaking to matters.

## LinkedIn Post Style
- Open with a hook that is either a question, a contradiction, or a quiet observation — not a humblebrag.
- Short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
- No bullet-point dumps. If it's a list, it earns its place.
- End with something that invites reflection — not always a call to action.
- Personal but not oversharing. Specific but not name-dropping.

## What to Avoid
- Hollow phrases: "excited to announce", "humbled and grateful", "thrilled to share"
- Performative vulnerability
- Metro-centric assumptions
- Overexplaining. Trust the reader.
